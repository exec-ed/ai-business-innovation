Facilitator Guide: AI-Driven Business Innovation Masterclass

A complete guide to delivering this 1-day AI-specific strategic workshop

Quick Overview

Course: AI-Driven Business Innovation
Duration: 7.5 hours (9:00 AM - 4:30 PM)
Audience: Executives, strategists, innovation leaders, portfolio managers
Format: Strategic frameworks + AI-specific investment criteria + Dragon’s Den simulation
Core Learning: AI investments require DIFFERENT evaluation than traditional IT

What makes this AI-specific: - Data readiness assessment (AI needs data, IT doesn’t) - Continuous learning costs (AI ongoing costs 30-50% vs. IT 10-15%) - Accuracy vs. risk tolerance (AI is probabilistic, IT is deterministic) - Explainability requirements (AI black box vs. IT traceable) - Ethical risk evaluation (AI amplifies bias, IT reflects human bias)

The “Aha!” Moment You’re Building Toward

Morning: Participants learn traditional strategic frameworks (Three Horizons, portfolio balance, ROI)

Afternoon Dragon’s Den: Participants discover that AI-specific criteria COMPLETELY CHANGE which projects to fund

The reveal: - Dynamic Pricing: 253% ROI (best!) → But $1.3M (over budget) + HIGH ethical risk (price discrimination lawsuits) - Inventory: 264% ROI (best!) → But $1.25M (over budget) + needs explainability for buyers - Fraud Detection: 315% ROI (best!) → But HIGH bias risk + needs 6-month data prep first - Chatbot: 92% ROI (worst) → But $525K (fits budget) + LOW risk → Maybe smartest choice?

Learning: Traditional ROI analysis would fund the WRONG projects. AI-specific criteria reveal hidden costs and existential risks.

Detailed Session Guide

SESSION 1: AI Strategy Foundations (9:00-10:30 AM)

9:00-9:15 AM: Welcome & Strategic Context (15 min)

Your Opening (5 min): “Welcome to AI-Driven Business Innovation. By the end of today, you’ll be able to answer a critical question: How is evaluating AI investments different from traditional IT investments?

We’re not here to learn how AI works technically. We’re here to learn how to make BETTER STRATEGIC DECISIONS about AI investments.

Here’s what you’ll discover today: The traditional strategic frameworks you know—Three Horizons, portfolio management, ROI analysis—are NECESSARY but NOT SUFFICIENT for AI. AI requires additional evaluation criteria. And in the Dragon’s Den this afternoon, you’ll see how these AI-specific criteria completely change which projects you should fund.”

Icebreaker Activity (10 min): “AI Initiatives Roundtable”

Instructions to group: “Let’s go around the room. Share: 1. Your name and role 2. One AI initiative your organisation has tried or is considering 3. One concern you have about AI investment

Keep it to 30 seconds each.”

Your role: - Listen for patterns (data quality issues? bias concerns? unclear ROI?) - Note common themes on flipchart - Keep moving (30 sec per person max)

Transition (capture themes): “I heard several common themes: [X, Y, Z]. Many of you mentioned [data quality / unclear ROI / bias concerns]. These are EXACTLY the AI-specific issues we’ll address today with our evaluation frameworks.”

9:15-9:45 AM: What Makes AI Different? (30 min)

This is THE KEY DIFFERENTIATOR of your course

Your Setup: “Before we dive into strategic frameworks, we need to answer: Why can’t we just use our normal IT investment frameworks for AI? What makes AI special?”

Present the 5 Ways AI is Different (5 min each):

1. Data Dependency (5 min)

“Traditional IT works without data. Your inventory alert system (‘if stock < 10, send alert’) works whether you have historical data or not.

AI is USELESS without quality training data. An AI inventory optimization system needs YEARS of sales history, and if that data is bad, the AI will be bad. No amount of algorithmic sophistication fixes garbage data.

Key point: Before funding any AI project, ask: ‘Is our data ready?’ We’ll use a 0-10 scoring system. Projects scoring <7 on data readiness should NOT be funded until data infrastructure is built first.”

Show example: RetailFlow chatbot scenario—has 2 years of customer inquiry data → data ready. But Fraud Detection has inconsistent fraud labels → needs 6-month data cleaning project first.

2. Continuous Learning (5 min)

“Traditional IT: Build once, maintain occasionally. Annual maintenance costs: 10-15% of development cost.

AI: Must continuously retrain as the world changes. Annual ongoing costs: 30-50% of development cost.

Key point: AI Year 1 budget is only 30% of true cost. Calculate 5-year TCO, not just development cost.”

Show example: Dynamic Pricing AI—needs real-time learning as market conditions change. Ongoing cost: $340K/year on top of $850K initial investment. Over 5 years: $2.5M total, not $850K.

3. Probabilistic Nature (5 min)

“Traditional IT is deterministic: Same input = same output, 100% of the time.

AI is probabilistic: ‘I’m 87% confident this customer will churn.’ And that 13% where it’s wrong? Could be catastrophic if it’s a high-stakes decision.

Key point: Match AI accuracy to business risk tolerance. Healthcare needs 99%+, marketing can accept 80%.”

Show example: Fraud Detection—false positive (block legitimate customer) vs. false negative (allow fraud). Need human-in-loop for medium-confidence scores.

4. Black Box Problem (5 min)

“Traditional IT: Developers can trace every decision step-by-step. ‘The system denied the loan because income < $50K AND credit score < 650.’

AI (especially deep learning): Input → Black Box → Output. ‘The model learned that pattern’ (but which pattern?).

Key point: Some industries REQUIRE explainability (banking, healthcare). This limits which AI algorithms you can use, which affects performance and cost.”

Show example: Dynamic Pricing—merchandising team must understand WHY prices changed. Need explainability dashboards (+$100K to budget).

5. Ethical & Bias Risks (5 min)

“Traditional IT: Biases exist but are explicit human rules. Easy to find and fix.

AI: Learns biases from historical data and applies them at massive scale. One biased AI = millions of biased decisions = existential reputational risk.

Key point: Customer-facing AI requires mandatory bias testing, diverse development teams, and ongoing monitoring. Budget 10-20% for ethics.”

Show example: Fraud Detection—could discriminate against protected demographics if not carefully tested. Dynamic Pricing—could charge different prices to different groups (illegal). Both need extensive bias testing.

Summary (3 min): “So AI requires FIVE additional evaluation criteria beyond traditional ROI: 1. Data readiness (0-10 score) 2. Continuous learning plan & costs 3. Accuracy vs. risk tolerance matching 4. Explainability requirements 5. Ethical risk assessment

This afternoon in Dragon’s Den, you’ll see how these criteria completely change which projects look attractive. Projects with the highest traditional ROI often have the worst AI-specific risk profiles.”

Show table (write on flipchart or slide):

                Traditional ROI    After AI-Specific Analysis
Chatbot         92% ROI           75% ROI, Low risk ✅
Dynamic Pricing 253% ROI (best!)  Over budget + HIGH ethical risk ⚠️
Inventory       264% ROI (best!)  Over budget slightly ⚠️
Fraud Detection 315% ROI (best!)  HIGH bias risk ⚠️

“Which would you fund if you only looked at ROI? Which should you actually fund?”

Transition: “Let’s apply this thinking to YOUR organizations. Exercise 1: Assess your AI portfolio AND your data readiness.”

9:45-10:30 AM: Exercise 1 - AI Tech Radar with Data Readiness (45 min)

Setup (5 min): “You’ll work in table groups. Your task: Map your organization’s AI initiatives on the AI Transformation Matrix, then assess data readiness for each.”

Distribute materials: - AI capability cards (12 cards showing different AI types) - Tech radar template (large format for group work) - Data readiness scorecard

Instructions: 1. AI Capability Mapping (15 min): - Review the 12 AI capability cards - Place each on the Transformation Matrix (Process vs. Strategic, Incremental vs. Transformational) - Identify where your organisation is currently active - Identify gaps

  1. Data Readiness Assessment (15 min):
    • For each AI capability where you’re active (or want to be), score data readiness 0-10:
      • Data Availability: Do you have enough historical data? (0-10)
      • Data Quality: Is it accurate, complete, consistent? (0-10)
      • Data Access: Can you integrate necessary sources? (0-10)
      • Data Bias: Is historical data representative? (0-10)
    • Average the 4 scores → Data Readiness Score
    • Flag anything <7 as “Data infrastructure needed first”
  2. Ethical Risk Flags (10 min):
    • For customer-facing AI, flag ethical risks:
      • Could this discriminate against protected groups?
      • Does this make life-impacting decisions?
    • Mark HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW ethical risk

Your role during exercise: - Circulate among tables - Push them on data readiness: “Do you REALLY have clean, unbiased historical data?” - Challenge optimism: “You scored 9/10 on data quality—have you actually audited it?” - Flag ethical risks they’re missing: “That’s customer-facing—have you considered bias?”

Common issues you’ll see: - Over-optimism on data: “We have data in SAP” ≠ “Data is AI-ready” - Push back: “Is it cleaned? Integrated? Sufficient volume? Unbiased?” - Ignoring ethical risks: They don’t flag customer-facing AI as high risk - Remind: “This makes decisions about customers—what if it’s biased?” - Clustering in OPTIMIZE quadrant: 80% of initiatives are process automation - Point out: “You’re over-invested in Optimize. Where’s your strategic AI?”

Debrief (5 min): Ask 2-3 tables to share: - “Where are you over-invested? Under-invested?” - “What surprised you about your data readiness scores?” - “Which initiatives can’t move forward until data infrastructure improves?”

Key learning to emphasize: “Most organisations are Level 2-3 on data maturity but trying to do Level 5 AI. That’s like trying to build a skyscraper on a foundation meant for a house. Data readiness gates AI investment decisions.”

BREAK (10:30-11:00 AM)

Use this time to: - Review how Exercise 1 went (running on time?) - Prepare flipcharts for Exercise 2 - Chat with participants 1-on-1 about their specific AI challenges

SESSION 2: Strategic Frameworks (11:00-12:30 PM)

11:00-11:30 AM: Data Value Pyramid & Three Horizons (30 min)

Framework 1: Data Value Pyramid (12 min)

Your intro: “Exercise 1 revealed that data readiness matters. But what does ‘AI-ready data’ actually look like? The Data Value Pyramid shows the journey from collecting data to autonomous AI operations.”

Present the 5 levels (2 min each):

  • Level 1: Data Collection - Raw data from operations
    • Example: Recording sales transactions, customer inquiries
    • AI capability: None yet (just collecting)
  • Level 2: Data Integration - Connecting data sources
    • Example: Sales + inventory + customer data in one place
    • AI capability: Basic reporting, simple dashboards
  • Level 3: Descriptive Analytics - Understanding what happened
    • Example: Sales trends, customer segments, demand patterns
    • AI capability: Pattern recognition, clustering, segmentation
  • Level 4: Predictive Analytics - Forecasting what will happen
    • Example: Demand forecasting, churn prediction, fraud detection
    • AI capability: Machine learning models, recommendations
  • Level 5: Prescriptive/Autonomous - AI decides and acts
    • Example: Dynamic pricing, automated inventory ordering
    • AI capability: Autonomous decision-making, continuous optimization

The gate principle (3 min): “You CANNOT skip levels. If you’re at Level 2 (disconnected data sources), you cannot do Level 5 AI (autonomous decisions). You’ll fail.

Most organisations are at Level 2-3 but want Level 5 results. That’s why 85% of AI projects fail—not because the AI is bad, but because the data foundation isn’t there.”

Show RetailFlow examples (3 min): - Chatbot: Needs Level 3 (historical inquiry patterns) → RetailFlow is ready - Inventory Optimization: Needs Level 4 (predictive forecasting) → RetailFlow is Level 3-4, ready - Dynamic Pricing: Needs Level 5 (real-time autonomous decisions) → RetailFlow is Level 3, NOT READY

Key takeaway: “Before funding any AI, ask: What data maturity level does this require? What level are we at? If there’s a gap, fund data infrastructure FIRST.”

Framework 2: Three Horizons Model (12 min)

Your intro: “Now that we know to match AI ambition to data maturity, let’s talk about balancing your AI portfolio across time horizons.”

Present the 3 horizons (3 min each):

  • Horizon 1: Optimize Core (0-12 months, 60-70% of budget)
    • Focus: Improve existing operations
    • ROI: >200% in Year 1
    • Risk: Low (proven technology)
    • Examples: Process automation, chatbots, basic forecasting
    • Data: Level 3-4 sufficient
  • Horizon 2: Build Emerging (1-3 years, 20-30% of budget)
    • Focus: Adjacent opportunities, new capabilities
    • ROI: >150% over 3 years
    • Risk: Medium (scaling challenges)
    • Examples: Personalization, dynamic pricing, supply chain AI
    • Data: Level 4-5 required
  • Horizon 3: Transform Future (3-5+ years, 10-15% of budget)
    • Focus: New business models, industry disruption
    • ROI: >100% over 5 years
    • Risk: High (breakthrough potential)
    • Examples: AI-powered products, autonomous operations, platform models
    • Data: Level 5 + continuous learning required

The portfolio balance principle (3 min): “Most organisations get this wrong in one of two ways: 1. All H1 (80%+ in optimize): Efficient today, irrelevant tomorrow. Competitors will transform while you optimize into obsolescence. 2. All H3 (50%+ in moonshots): Exciting vision, no cash flow today. Can’t fund transformation if core business suffers.

The right balance: 60-70% H1, 20-30% H2, 10-15% H3.”

Show RetailFlow examples (3 min): - Chatbot: H1 (optimize customer service) - Fraud Detection: H1 (protect core business) - Dynamic Pricing: H2 (build new capability, competitive advantage) - Inventory Optimization: H1-H2 (optimize + build capability)

“With $2M budget, we need BALANCE. Can’t spend it all on one H2 project (inventory at $1.1M). Need mix of H1 + H2.”

Key takeaway: “Evaluate your AI portfolio: Are you balanced? Too much H1 = no future. Too much H3 = no present. Find the right mix.”

Transition: “Let’s apply Three Horizons thinking to YOUR competitive landscape. Exercise 2: Where are threats and opportunities? Which horizons are they in?”

11:30-12:30 PM: Exercise 2 - Threat & Opportunity Mapping (60 min)

Setup (5 min): “This is a competitive intelligence exercise. You’ll identify how AI could threaten your business AND where AI creates opportunities, then position them on the Three Horizons model.”

Distribute materials: - Large flipchart sheets with Three Horizons grid pre-drawn - Sticky notes (red for threats, green for opportunities) - Markers

Phase 1: Threat Mapping (15 min) “On RED sticky notes, write threats: - How could competitors use AI against you? - How could AI startups disrupt your industry? - What happens if you DON’T invest in AI and others do?

Write one threat per sticky note. Be specific. Place on appropriate horizon: - H1 threats: Happening now (competitors already doing it) - H2 threats: Emerging (some experimentation, not scaled yet) - H3 threats: Future (possible but not yet real)

Example threats: - H1: ‘Competitor launches chatbot, we still use phone-only support’ - H2: ‘Online-first retailers using dynamic pricing, we’re static’ - H3: ‘AI-powered startups with no stores, fully autonomous operations’”

Your role: - Push them to be specific: “Not just ‘AI will disrupt us’—HOW specifically?” - Challenge assumptions: “You think that’s H3? Isn’t someone already doing it?” (If so, it’s H2 or H1) - Make it personal: “What keeps YOU up at night about AI competition?”

Phase 2: Opportunity Mapping (15 min) “On GREEN sticky notes, write opportunities: - Where could AI create competitive advantage? - What new capabilities could AI enable? - What customer problems could AI solve better?

Place on appropriate horizon. Be realistic about timeframe and difficulty.”

Your role: - Push for strategic thinking: “That’s incremental—where’s the transformational AI?” - Reality-check data readiness: “Cool idea—but is your data ready for that?” - Link to morning learning: “What data maturity level does that need? Are you there?”

Phase 3: Data Readiness Check (10 min - NEW) “For each GREEN opportunity sticky note, write a number 1-10 on the corner: Your data readiness score for that opportunity.

Be honest. Use the criteria from Exercise 1: - Do you have historical data? - Is it good quality? - Can you access/integrate it? - Is it unbiased?

Any scored <7? That opportunity needs data infrastructure investment FIRST.”

Phase 4: Group Presentations (15 min) 3 groups × 5 min each

Ask each group: 1. “What’s your biggest H1 threat?” (happening now) 2. “What’s your most exciting opportunity?” 3. “Which opportunities are blocked by data readiness?”

Your facilitation: - Look for patterns: Are all opportunities in H1? (No transformational thinking) - Highlight data readiness gaps: “4 of your 6 opportunities scored <7. Data is your bottleneck.” - Connect to Dragon’s Den: “This afternoon you’ll see how data readiness affects investment decisions.”

Debrief (5 min): “What themes do you see? - Threats tend to be H1-H2 (real and coming fast) - Opportunities tend to be H2-H3 (aspirational) - Data readiness is often LOW for big opportunities (not ready yet)

The strategic question: Do you invest in quick H1 wins while you build data infrastructure? Or pause AI ambitions until data is ready?

There’s no one right answer, but you must be CONSCIOUS of this trade-off. This afternoon in Dragon’s Den, you’ll face exactly this dilemma.”

LUNCH BREAK (12:30-1:15 PM)

Important: Check your timing. Are you on track? - If behind: Plan to shorten Dragon’s Den prep to 35 min (cut 10 min) - If ahead: Use extra time in Dragon’s Den debrief

SESSION 3: AI Investment Framework & Dragon’s Den Setup (1:15-2:30 PM)

1:15-1:45 PM: AI Investment Model (30 min)

Your intro: “This morning you learned AI requires different evaluation criteria. This afternoon, you’ll use the AI Investment Model to make real portfolio allocation decisions. This framework combines traditional ROI analysis WITH AI-specific criteria.”

Part A: Traditional Investment Criteria (10 min)

Quickly review (they know this): 1. Four Value Categories: - Cost reduction (labor, efficiency, waste) - Revenue growth (new sales, retention, pricing power) - Risk reduction (fraud, compliance, quality) - Strategic positioning (competitive advantage, capabilities)

  1. Three Horizons Fit:
    • H1: >200% ROI Year 1
    • H2: >150% ROI over 3 years
    • H3: >100% ROI over 5 years
  2. Strategic Fit:
    • Portfolio balance (70/20/10 across horizons)
    • Transformation Matrix position
    • Capability building

“Traditional criteria answer: Is this a good investment? But for AI, that’s only HALF the question.”

Part B: AI-Specific Criteria (15 min - THE KEY SECTION)

Introduce the AI Investment Checklist: “This checklist has TWO sections. Section A: Traditional criteria (you just reviewed). Section B: FIVE AI-specific criteria that often disqualify projects that look great on traditional ROI.

Let’s walk through the 5 AI-specific criteria using our Dragon’s Den scenarios.”

1. Data Readiness Score (0-10, need 7+) (3 min)

“Score across 4 dimensions: - Data availability (enough volume?) - Data quality (accurate, complete?) - Data access (can integrate sources?) - Data bias (representative?)

Average the 4 scores. If <7, STOP. Fund data infrastructure first, THEN AI.”

RetailFlow examples: - Chatbot: 8/10 (ready ✅) - Fraud Detection: 6/10 (needs 6-month data labeling project first ⚠️) - Dynamic Pricing: 6/10 (needs competitor data sourcing ⚠️) - Inventory: 7/10 (ready with some cleaning ✅)

“See how Fraud Detection, despite 315% ROI, can’t start yet? Data isn’t ready.”

2. Continuous Learning Plan (30-50% ongoing costs) (3 min)

“AI isn’t build-once. Need retraining as world changes. Three approaches: - Static AI: Train once, 10-15% annual cost (but becomes obsolete) - Batch retraining: Quarterly/monthly updates, 25-30% annual cost - Real-time learning: Continuous adaptation, 35-50% annual cost

Calculate 5-year TCO, not just Year 1 cost.”

Show table:

Project         Year 1    Annual Ongoing    5-Year TCO
Chatbot         $525K     $158K (30%)      $1.2M
Dynamic Pricing $1.3M     $520K (40%)      $3.4M (!!)
Inventory       $1.25M    $375K (30%)      $2.75M
Fraud           $1.05M    $420K (40%)      $2.7M

“See how ongoing costs add up? Dynamic Pricing looks like $1.3M but is actually $3.4M over 5 years.”

3. Accuracy vs. Risk Tolerance (3 min)

“AI is probabilistic. Match requirements to capability: - Low stakes (recommendations): 70-80% accuracy OK - Medium stakes (operations): 85-95% accuracy needed - High stakes (legal, medical, financial): 95-99%+ required

If AI can’t meet accuracy requirement, add human-in-loop OR don’t proceed.”

RetailFlow examples: - Chatbot: 90-95% achievable, medium stakes → OK with escalation process ✅ - Fraud Detection: 95-97% achievable, but two-sided risk (false positive hurts customers) → Need human review for medium-confidence cases ⚠️ - Dynamic Pricing: 98%+ needed (pricing errors hurt brand), AI achieves 95% → Need guardrails ⚠️

4. Explainability Requirements (regulatory + operational) (3 min)

“Can you explain WHY the AI made that decision?

Three levels: - HIGH (regulated industries): Must explain legally → Use interpretable models only (limits algorithm choice) - MEDIUM (operational trust): Need to understand for adoption → Add explanation tools - LOW (consumer-facing): Results speak for themselves → Any algorithm OK

High explainability = +10-20% budget + limits to simpler algorithms.”

RetailFlow examples: - Dynamic Pricing: HIGH (merchandisers must understand why prices changed) → Need explainability dashboards (+$100K) ⚠️ - Fraud Detection: HIGH (must explain to customers why blocked) → Need audit trails (+$100K) ⚠️ - Inventory: MEDIUM (buyers need to trust recommendations) → Add explainability (+$75K) ⚠️ - Chatbot: LOW (customers just want answers) → No additional cost ✅

5. Ethical Risk Assessment (bias + discrimination) (3 min)

“Could this AI discriminate against protected groups? Three risk levels: - HIGH (life-impacting): Hiring, lending, medical → Extensive testing required - MEDIUM (customer-facing): Pricing, recommendations → Bias testing needed - LOW (internal operations): Inventory, logistics → Basic review

High risk = +20-30% budget for bias testing, diverse teams, monitoring.”

RetailFlow examples: - Dynamic Pricing: HIGH (could charge different prices to different groups = illegal) → Need legal review + bias testing (+$150K) ⚠️ - Fraud Detection: HIGH (could target minorities unfairly) → Need external bias audit (+$125K) ⚠️ - Inventory: LOW (internal operations) → Basic fairness check (+$25K) ✅ - Chatbot: LOW-MEDIUM (customer service) → Bias testing (+$25K) ✅

Summary: The AI Investment Decision Tree (5 min)

Show on flipchart/slide:

Traditional Criteria
├── Strategic fit? (Horizons, Matrix, Portfolio)
├── Financial ROI? (4 value categories)
└── Feasible? (Resources, timeline)
    └── YES → Proceed to AI-Specific Criteria

AI-Specific Criteria
├── Data readiness ≥7? (If NO: Stop, fund data first)
├── Continuous learning plan? (Add 30-50% to annual costs)
├── Accuracy matches risk? (If NO: Add human-in-loop or stop)
├── Explainability addressed? (Add tools or limit algorithms)
└── Ethical risks mitigated? (Add bias testing + diverse teams)
    └── ALL YES → APPROVED

“Both sets must be satisfied. High ROI with bad data readiness? REJECT. Low ethical risk but poor ROI? REJECT. Need BOTH.”

The Dragon’s Den Preview: “In 30 minutes, you’ll evaluate 4 projects: - Chatbot: Lowest ROI (92%) but passes all AI criteria → safe choice - Dynamic Pricing: Highest ROI (253%) but FAILS multiple AI criteria (over budget, high ethical risk) → risky choice - Inventory: High ROI (264%) but exceeds budget cap → trim or reject? - Fraud Detection: High ROI (315%) but data not ready + high bias risk → delay 6 months?

Using only traditional ROI, you’d fund Dynamic Pricing, Inventory, and Fraud Detection. Using AI-specific criteria, those three all have major red flags. The ‘worst’ ROI project (Chatbot) might actually be the smartest choice.

That’s the power of AI-specific evaluation.”

1:45-2:30 PM: Dragon’s Den Setup & Team Preparation (45 min)

Setup (10 min)

Form teams: “We’re dividing into two groups:

Investment Committee (4-5 people): - You represent RetailFlow’s executive team (CEO, CFO, CTO, CMO) - Your job: Evaluate 4 AI proposals and allocate $2M budget - You have the AI Investment Checklist—use it ruthlessly - You MUST consider both traditional ROI AND AI-specific criteria

Project Teams (remaining participants, 3 teams of 3-4): - Team 1: Pitch the Chatbot ($450K original, $525K with AI costs) - Team 2: Pitch Dynamic Pricing ($850K original, $1.3M with AI costs) - Team 3: Pitch Inventory Optimization ($1.1M original, $1.25M with AI costs)

Fraud Detection will be presented by me (pre-prepared pitch).

Constraints: - Total budget: $2M (cannot exceed) - Must fund at least 2 initiatives (diversification) - Maximum $1.2M for any single project - Timeline: All must show results within 18 months”

Distribute materials: - Investment Committee: AI Investment Checklist (1 per person), scoring sheets - Project Teams: Pitch scenario with AI-specific sections, blank pitch template, ROI calculator

The pitch structure (explain to project teams): “Your 7-minute pitch must cover: 1. The business problem (30 sec) 2. Traditional ROI (cost reduction, revenue growth, risk, strategic) (2 min) 3. NEW: AI-Specific Criteria (3 min) - Data readiness score (show your work) - Continuous learning plan (5-year TCO) - Accuracy vs. risk tolerance - Explainability approach - Ethical risk mitigation 4. Why you should be funded (1.5 min)

Committee will grill you on AI-specific risks. Be ready to defend.”

Key instructions for Investment Committee: “Your job is to be SKEPTICAL. Push on: - ‘Your data readiness is 7/10—what’s the 3 points you’re missing?’ - ‘You claim low ethical risk—have you tested for bias?’ - ‘Ongoing costs are $400K/year—can we afford that?’ - ‘This exceeds the $1.2M cap—can you trim scope?’

Use the AI Investment Checklist. Any project that FAILS an AI-specific criterion should be flagged.”

Team Preparation (35 min)

Your role: - Circulate among project teams - Push them to address AI-specific criteria honestly: - “Don’t lowball the continuous learning costs—be realistic” - “Don’t ignore ethical risks—committee will catch it” - “If your data readiness is <7, admit it and explain the mitigation plan” - Help Investment Committee prepare tough questions: - “What AI-specific question concerns you most?” - “Which criterion is most likely to disqualify a project?” - “How will you handle the budget constraint?”

Common issues you’ll see: - Project teams underestimate AI costs: “We think ongoing costs are only 15%” - Push back: “The scenario says 30-40%. Don’t wishful-think your way past this.” - Project teams ignore ethical risks: “Our fraud detection has no bias issues” - Challenge: “Have you tested across demographics? How do you know?” - Investment Committee focuses only on ROI: “We should just fund highest ROI” - Remind: “What about the AI-specific criteria? Dynamic Pricing has high ROI but also high ethical risk.”

5 minutes before break: “Teams, you have 5 minutes to finalise your pitch. After break, we start. Committee, review your questions.”

BREAK (2:30-3:00 PM)

Use this time to: - Set up the “Dragon’s Den” room arrangement (committee at front, project teams present) - Prepare your Fraud Detection pitch (you’ll deliver it) - Check: Is any team unprepared? Give quick coaching if needed

SESSION 4: Dragon’s Den & Strategic Synthesis (3:00-4:30 PM)

3:00-4:10 PM: Exercise 3 - Dragon’s Den Simulation (70 min)

Your opening (2 min): “Welcome to the RetailFlow Investment Committee meeting. CEO Sarah Martinez has given us $2M to invest in AI this year. We have 4 proposals. Our job: Evaluate using BOTH traditional ROI AND AI-specific criteria, then allocate budget strategically.

Remember: We cannot fund everything. Maximum $1.2M per project. Must fund at least 2 projects.

Let’s begin.”

Pitch 1: Chatbot (15 min total)

  • 7 min: Team pitch
  • 5 min: Committee Q&A
    • Questions to probe:
      • “Your data readiness is 8/10—what’s the gap?”
      • “You have LOW ethical risk—have you tested for bias in customer treatment?”
      • “Continuous learning is $158K/year—can you justify that?”
  • 3 min: Committee private scoring (use AI Investment Checklist)

Your facilitation: - Keep time strictly (use visible timer) - If committee doesn’t ask about AI-specific criteria, prompt: “Committee, what about explainability? Ethical risks?” - Take notes on flipchart: ROI, Budget, Key risks

Pitch 2: Dynamic Pricing (15 min total)

  • 7 min: Team pitch
  • 5 min: Committee Q&A
    • Questions to probe:
      • “You’re at $1.3M—that EXCEEDS the $1.2M cap. What would you cut?”
      • “HIGH ethical risk—could you face price discrimination lawsuits?”
      • “Explainability requirements add $100K—is that enough?”
      • “Ongoing costs are $520K/year—that’s 40% of Year 1. Can we sustain that?”
  • 3 min: Committee scoring

Your facilitation: - This project looks great on traditional ROI but terrible on AI-specific criteria - Make sure committee catches: Over budget + high ethical risk + high explainability needs - If committee loves this project based on ROI alone, interject: “Committee, I notice you haven’t discussed the ethical risk section. The scenario flags HIGH risk of price discrimination. How do you assess that?”

Pitch 3: Inventory Optimization (15 min total)

  • 7 min: Team pitch
  • 5 min: Committee Q&A
    • Questions to probe:
      • “You’re at $1.25M—exceeds the $1.2M cap by $50K. Can you trim?”
      • “Data readiness is 7/10—what’s the gap and how will you fix it?”
      • “MEDIUM explainability needs—buyers must trust the system. How do you ensure adoption?”
      • “Ongoing costs $375K/year—what if budget gets cut?”
  • 3 min: Committee scoring

Your facilitation: - This project is close to fundable (good ROI, manageable AI risks) - Key issue: Slightly over budget cap - Watch for: Does committee prioritize this over chatbot (higher ROI) or worry about the budget overage?

Pitch 4: Fraud Detection (10 min total) - YOU PRESENT THIS

Your pitch (7 min): “I’m presenting Fraud Detection on behalf of the Security team.

Traditional ROI: $1.05M investment, $3.2M/year in prevented fraud, 315% ROI. Highest ROI of all projects.

AI-Specific Criteria: 1. Data Readiness: 6/10 ⚠️ - Our fraud labels are inconsistent. We need a 6-month data labeling project ($75K) BEFORE we can train the AI. This delays the project timeline. 2. Continuous Learning: Real-time ($420K/year) - Fraudsters adapt constantly. We must retrain continuously or become obsolete. 3. Accuracy vs. Risk: Two-sided risk - False positives anger legitimate customers. False negatives let fraud through. Need human-in-loop for medium-confidence cases. 4. Explainability: HIGH ⚠️ - We must explain to customers why they were blocked. Need audit trails. +$100K. 5. Ethical Risk: HIGH ⚠️ - Fraud detection AI has a history of racial bias. We need external bias audit before launch. +$125K.

Revised budget: $1.05M (already over budget, and that’s AFTER adding AI-specific costs) Timeline: Cannot start for 6 months (data prep required) Risk: HIGH ethical risk + regulatory risk

Despite highest ROI, this project has significant AI-specific red flags. Recommend: Delay until data is ready, OR fund data prep now and AI development in Year 2.”

Committee Q&A (3 min): Take questions from committee. Defend the AI-specific concerns honestly.

Your facilitation: - This reveals the core learning: Highest ROI ≠ best AI investment - Make sure committee understands: Can’t start for 6 months, high bias risk, expensive ongoing costs

Committee Deliberation (10 min)

“Committee, you’ve heard all 4 pitches. You have $2M to allocate. You must decide: - Which projects to fund? - How much to each? - Which to reject or delay?

Use your AI Investment Checklist. Consider BOTH traditional ROI AND AI-specific criteria.

You have 10 minutes. Deliberate and decide.”

Your role: - Stay out of it (let them struggle with the trade-offs) - If they’re only talking about ROI, prompt: “What about the AI-specific criteria?” - If they ignore ethical risks, prompt: “Committee, I haven’t heard discussion of the ethical risks. Should that factor in?” - Watch for: Are they being rigorous with the AI Investment Checklist, or just going with gut feel?

Investment Decision Announcement (5 min)

“Committee, please announce your allocation decisions and rationale.”

Listen for: - Which projects did they fund? Why? - Did AI-specific criteria change their thinking? - Did they address ethical risks? - Did they stay within budget?

Common outcomes you’ll see: 1. Risk-averse: Fund Chatbot ($525K) + Inventory (trimmed to $1.2M) = $1.725M (under budget, low risk) 2. ROI-focused: Try to fund Inventory ($1.25M) + Fraud ($1.05M) = $2.3M (OVER BUDGET, high risk) 3. Balanced: Fund Chatbot ($525K) + Inventory (trimmed to $1.2M) + Fraud data prep only ($75K) = $1.8M

No matter what they decide, there’s learning: - If they funded Dynamic Pricing: “You funded a project with HIGH ethical risk and over budget. What’s your mitigation plan for price discrimination lawsuits?” - If they funded only Chatbot: “You chose the lowest ROI project. Was that wise given the other options?” - If they tried to fund everything: “You exceeded the $2M budget. In real life, CFO says no. What gets cut?”

4:10-4:25 PM: Debrief & Key Insights (15 min)

This is where the learning is consolidated. Don’t rush this.

Ask the room:

  1. “How did AI-specific criteria change your evaluation?” (5 min)
    • Listen for: “We wanted to fund Dynamic Pricing for ROI, but ethical risks scared us off”
    • Listen for: “Fraud Detection looked great until we saw data wasn’t ready”
    • Listen for: “Chatbot has lowest ROI but fewest red flags”
  2. “What surprised you about the ‘hidden costs’?” (3 min)
    • Prompt: “Ongoing costs were 30-50%, not 10-15% like traditional IT”
    • Prompt: “Bias testing added $100-150K we didn’t expect”
    • Prompt: “Data prep (6 months for fraud) delayed highest ROI project”
  3. “Which project would you have funded if you ONLY looked at traditional ROI?” (2 min)
    • Most will say: Dynamic Pricing (253%), Inventory (264%), Fraud (315%)
    • Point out: All three have major AI-specific issues
  4. “How will you apply this to your real AI investments?” (5 min)
    • Listen for specific commitments:
      • “I’m going to audit our data readiness before approving any AI”
      • “I need to add ethical risk assessment to our approval process”
      • “I’m going to recalculate our AI TCO with realistic ongoing costs”
    • Capture these on flipchart

Your summary: “Here’s what you learned today:

AI investments require DIFFERENT evaluation than traditional IT: - Data readiness gates everything (6/10 = not ready, delay the project) - Ongoing costs are 3-5x higher than traditional IT (30-50% vs. 10-15%) - Ethical risks are existential (one biased AI = massive reputational damage) - Explainability isn’t optional in regulated industries (adds cost + limits algorithms) - Highest ROI doesn’t mean best investment (must pass AI-specific criteria too)

The strategic question for Monday: When your team proposes an AI investment, ask: 1. Is our data ready? (score 0-10) 2. What’s the 5-year TCO? (not just Year 1) 3. What’s the cost of being wrong? (match accuracy to risk) 4. Must we explain decisions? (regulatory? operational?) 5. Could this discriminate? (test for bias)

If you can’t answer all 5, you’re not ready to invest.”

4:25-4:30 PM: Personal Action Planning (5 min)

“Take 5 minutes. In your Personal Action Plan template, write:

  1. One AI initiative you’ll evaluate differently starting Monday
  2. Your organisation’s data readiness score (0-10, be honest)
  3. The AI-specific criterion that concerns you most (data? ethics? explainability?)
  4. Two actions you’ll take in the next 30 days:
    • Example: “Audit data readiness for our customer segmentation AI”
    • Example: “Add AI Investment Checklist to our approval process”
    • Example: “Commission bias testing for our pricing algorithm”
  5. Who you’ll share this with (your team? your boss? your board?)

You have 5 minutes. Go.”

Silent working time. Do not interrupt.

At 4:30: “Thank you for participating in AI-Driven Business Innovation. You now have frameworks to evaluate AI investments using BOTH traditional strategic criteria AND AI-specific criteria.

Your AI Investment Checklist is yours to keep. Use it Monday morning.

Safe travels, and good luck with your AI investments.”

Troubleshooting Guide

“We’re running 15 minutes behind after Exercise 1”

Solution: Shorten Exercise 2 to 45 min (cut group presentations to 10 min total)

“The Investment Committee isn’t asking about AI-specific criteria”

Solution: Interject after Pitch 1: “Committee, I notice you’re focused on ROI. The AI Investment Checklist has 5 AI-specific criteria—should those factor into your evaluation?”

“Project teams are ignoring ethical risks in their pitches”

Solution: During prep time, visit each team: “Your scenario flags HIGH ethical risk. How will you address that in your pitch?”

“Dynamic Pricing team is defensive about ethical concerns”

Solution: “That defensiveness is realistic—project advocates often minimise risks. Committee’s job is to probe those risks. Let’s see how they handle it.”

“Participants are skeptical: ‘This is too theoretical’”

Solution: Use real examples: “Amazon faced price discrimination lawsuit over dynamic pricing. That’s why ethical risk isn’t theoretical.”

“Committee wants to fund everything, ignores budget cap”

Solution: “In real life, CFO says ‘You have $2M, not $3M.’ What gets cut? You must make trade-offs.”

Key Success Factors

  1. Emphasize AI-specific criteria throughout: Don’t let participants default to traditional ROI thinking
  2. Let Dragon’s Den be messy: The struggle with trade-offs IS the learning
  3. Probe ethical risks relentlessly: This is what makes the course AI-specific
  4. Use RetailFlow realistically: Don’t let teams invent data they don’t have
  5. Connect to their context: “How does this apply to YOUR organization?”

You’re now ready to deliver this course. Trust the frameworks, push on AI-specific criteria, and let participants discover that AI investment is genuinely different.

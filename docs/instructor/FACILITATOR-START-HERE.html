AI-Driven Business Innovation Masterclass - 1 Day (9 AM - 4:30 PM)

Quick Navigation

Before the course: Read this file (20 min) 1 week before: Read all 3 exercise facilitator guides (1 hour total) Day before: Print materials, test tech, arrange room Morning of: Arrive 8:30 AM, set up, distribute materials During course: Use the 3 exercise guides as your playbook

What You’re Teaching

Morning: Strategic frameworks for AI investment - Exercise 1 (9:55-10:40): Where are you NOW (AI Tech Radar) - Exercise 2 (11:20-12:30): How to THINK about possibilities (AI Tools)

Afternoon: Make strategic investment decisions - Exercise 3 (2:45-3:55): Where should you invest (Dragon’s Den simulation)

The learning arc: Framework understanding → AI-augmented thinking → Investment decisions using both

The “Aha!” Moment

You’re building toward this discovery:

Morning: Participants learn traditional frameworks (ROI, portfolio balance, Three Horizons)

Afternoon Dragon’s Den: They apply frameworks and realise AI-specific criteria change which projects to fund

The insight: - Traditional IT: “Highest ROI wins” - AI Investment: “Highest ROI MIGHT LOSE if ethical risk is high or data prep takes 6 months”

This is the whole point. Let it emerge naturally.

The 4 Exercises at a Glance

Exercise 1: AI Tech Radar (45 min)

What: Map 12 AI capability cards onto matrix Why: Help teams SEE their current state (usually 80% Optimize, 20% Transform) Your job: Facilitate discussion, help them notice patterns, question if patterns are intentional

Read: exercise-1-facilitator-guide.md

Exercise 2: AI-Assisted Strategic Analysis (70 min)

What: Use ChatGPT/Claude/Gemini to challenge thinking Why: Show AI as strategic thought partner (not magic, not useless) Your job: Setup tech, model good prompting, celebrate “aha!” moments, manage time strictly

Read: exercise-2-facilitator-guide.md

Exercise 3: Dragon’s Den (70 min)

What: Investment committee simulation. Teams pitch 4 AI projects. Committee allocates $2M budget. Why: Apply ALL learning. Discover traditional ROI analysis would fund WRONG projects. Your job: Brief teams, moderate pitches, let committee debate, debrief on AI-specific thinking

Read: exercise-3-facilitator-guide.md

Exercise 4: Strategic Response Scenarios (15 min)

What: Three rapid-fire strategic decisions with anonymous voting (MS Forms) Why: Build framework muscle memory. Participants use frameworks automatically under time pressure. Your job: Read scenarios, time voting, facilitate discussion, celebrate framework thinking

Read: exercise-4-facilitator-guide.md When: 1:15 PM - 1:30 PM (post-lunch energizer, before Dragon’s Den team prep)

Facilitation Philosophy (Key to Success)

✅ Do This

Ask questions: - “What patterns do you notice?” - “Why do you think that happened?” - “What would change if…?”

Let them struggle: - Productive struggle teaches better than clear answers - Give them time to think

Celebrate insights: - “That’s exactly the learning.” - “Notice what just happened?”

Model good thinking: - If they ask a question, show HOW you’d find the answer - Don’t just give the answer

❌ Don’t Do This

Lecture: - “Here’s the right answer” - “Everyone should have X in their output” - Avoid 20+ min presentations

Dismiss skepticism: - “This doesn’t work” or “This is a waste” - Instead: “Interesting, why do you think that? Let’s see…”

Compare teams: - “Table 3 did this better” - Individual experiences matter; avoid ranking

Rush: - Let them sit with ambiguity - Silence is OK

Key Teaching Points (Weave In Naturally)

Exercise 1: - “Most organisations cluster in Optimize. Strategic leaders intentionally balance.” - “Transform feels risky because it IS. But the risk of ONLY optimizing is worse.”

Exercise 2: - “AI has breadth, you have depth. Together = better.” - “Better prompts = better insights. Prompt engineering is an executive skill.” - “AI can be confidently wrong. Your expertise evaluates what’s useful.”

Exercise 3: - “Notice how AI-specific criteria (data readiness, ethical risk, bias) changed your decision?” - “Traditional ROI would have funded the wrong project.” - “AI investment is different from traditional IT. Frameworks help you see why.”

Common Situations & How to Handle Them

“We don’t have time for this exercise”

Reality: Every exercise is essential to the learning arc.

If timing is tight: - Cut lunch (not exercises) - Run parallel Dragon’s Den pitches (2 teams pitch simultaneously) - Shorten debrief time, not exercise time

“My table isn’t engaging”

Options: 1. Circulate, ask specific questions, don’t move on 2. Make them “demo table”—they do exercise, others watch/discuss 3. For Exercise 2 internet issues: You demonstrate on projector, they observe and discuss

“Someone says ‘This is a waste of time’”

Script: “I hear you. Bear with me. [After exercise phase] Now that you’ve done it—did you learn anything? Did an idea change? Usually something emerges.”

Usually skeptics convert after seeing results.

“Teams are going too fast / too slow”

Too fast: - Add depth: “Ask AI a follow-up question” or “Discuss longer before moving on”

Too slow: - Extend deadline or skip optional components - For Exercise 3: Have pre-prepared debate example instead of live - Keep the core learning, skip elaboration

“Dragon’s Den pitches are running long”

  • Use strict timekeeping (1 min warning, “time’s up” at 7 min)
  • Skip Q&A if needed, go straight to committee deliberation
  • Still teaches the learning, just compresses timeline

Success Looks Like

During Exercise 1: - Tables discussing card placement (not silent) - Someone says: “Wow, we have NO Transform initiatives”

During Exercise 2: - Animated discussion after AI responses - At least one “aha!” moment per table - Someone asks follow-up prompts

During Exercise 3: - Pitches are passionate (teams invested) - Committee questions shift from ROI to risk/ethics - Someone says: “Wait, we’re not funding the highest ROI project?” - Debrief: “That’s because AI-specific criteria matter more”

This afternoon: - A Dragon’s Den team says: “Let’s ask Claude about our pitch” (without you suggesting it)

Final reflection: - “I’m using this framework at work Monday” - Reference to something AI helped them think about - “This is different from how we normally make decisions”

Troubleshooting Tech

No internet for Exercise 2: - You demonstrate on your laptop with projector - Teams watch, discuss what AI would say, still learn - Backup: Use pre-prepared AI responses from answer-keys file

Projector not working: - Pivot to discussion-based (no slides needed) - Use large flipcharts - You facilitate from the room, not from stage

No devices at table during Exercise 2: - Make them “demo table” - They watch your demo, others gather around - Still participates, still learns

One Final Thing

You’re not lecturing. You’re facilitating discovery.

The exercises do most of the teaching. Your job: - Ask good questions - Listen more than you talk - Notice insights, amplify them - Keep time, manage energy - Stay curious about how participants think

If someone has an insight you didn’t expect, that’s SUCCESS. Let it emerge.

Questions? Review the relevant exercise guide. Each one has troubleshooting and common scenarios.

Good luck. The learning usually takes care of itself if you get the facilitation right.

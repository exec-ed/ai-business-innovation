### 9:20-9:50 AM: Framework 1 - AI Transformation Matrix

**Teaching Approach (30 min):**

**Core Concept (10 min):**
Draw the matrix on flipchart:
```
               TRANSFORMATIONAL
    ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
    │ REVOLUTIONIZE│  TRANSFORM   │
────┼──────────────┼──────────────┼────
    │  OPTIMIZE    │   ENHANCE    │
    └──────────────┼──────────────┘
               INCREMENTAL
     PROCESS ←────────────→ STRATEGIC
```

**Key Message:**
"Not all AI initiatives are equal. Most organizations start in OPTIMIZE - efficiency gains. That's fine, but limiting. Strategic leaders balance across all four quadrants."

**Examples (15 min):**
- **Optimize**: RPA, automated data entry (save time/cost)
- **Enhance**: Recommendation systems (better customer experience)
- **Revolutionize**: Predictive maintenance (reinvent operations)
- **Transform**: AI-driven products (new business model)

**Ask participants:**
"From our icebreaker, where do your initiatives fall? I bet most are in OPTIMIZE..."

**Common Mistake to Highlight (5 min):**
"The biggest mistake: calling everything 'AI transformation' when it's really automation. Be honest about classification - helps set expectations and allocate resources appropriately."

**Transition to Exercise:**
"Now you'll map your organization's AI portfolio. Where are you over/under-invested? Let's find out..."

### 9:50-10:35 AM: EXERCISE 1 - AI Tech Radar

**Setup (5 min):**
1. Keep participants at tables (3-4 per table)
2. Distribute capability cards and matrix templates
3. Draw large matrix on flipchart for each table

**Instructions to Participants:**
"You have 45 minutes:
1. Review capability cards (10 min) - which does your org have or plan?
2. Plot on matrix (15 min) - place cards in appropriate quadrant
3. Gap analysis (5 min) - what's missing? where's the imbalance?
4. We'll debrief as a group (15 min)"

**Your Role During Exercise:**
- **Circulate constantly** - visit each table
- **Listen for insights** - note interesting observations to share
- **Answer questions** - help with classification edge cases
- **Keep time** - announce at 10 min, 25 min, 35 min marks

**Common Questions You'll Get:**

*Q: "Is a chatbot Optimize or Enhance?"*
A: "Depends on use. Customer service chatbot that automates existing process = Optimize. AI advisor that creates new customer experience = Enhance. What's the primary value?"

*Q: "We don't have any Transform initiatives. Is that bad?"*
A: "Not necessarily. Depends on your strategy and maturity. Horizon 1 (core business) is 60-70% of portfolio typically. But zero Transform might mean missed opportunities."

*Q: "What if we can't agree on classification?"*
A: "The debate IS the value. It forces clarity on what you're actually building. Pick one and document why."

**Debrief (15 min):**

**Ask each table:**
- "What surprised you about your portfolio?"
- "Where are you over-invested? Under-invested?"

**Key Insights to Surface:**
1. **Over-concentration in Optimize**: "I see most of you clustered in Optimize. That's normal - easiest to justify ROI. But is it strategic?"
2. **Empty Transform quadrant**: "Who has initiatives in Transform? [Usually few hands]. Why? That's where competitive advantage lives."
3. **Portfolio imbalance**: "Strategic leaders balance across quadrants. You don't need equal distribution, but you need INTENTIONAL distribution."

**Facilitator Note:**
*This exercise sets the foundation. They'll reference these classifications all day. Ensure they understand the matrix before moving on.*

### 10:50-11:20 AM: Framework 2 - Data Value Pyramid & Three Horizons

**Part A: Data Value Pyramid (15 min)**

**Core Concept:**
Draw pyramid on flipchart:
```
        ┌─────────────┐
        │ AUTONOMOUS  │ Level 5
        ├─────────────┤
        │ PRESCRIPTIVE│ Level 4
        ├─────────────┤
        │ PREDICTIVE  │ Level 3
        ├─────────────┤
        │ DIAGNOSTIC  │ Level 2
        ├─────────────┤
        │ DESCRIPTIVE │ Level 1
        └─────────────┘
```

**Key Message:**
"You can't jump to Level 5 (autonomous AI) if your data is at Level 1 (basic collection). AI success requires data foundation."

**Examples:**
- Level 1: "We have sales data" (what happened)
- Level 2: "We know why sales dropped" (why it happened)
- Level 3: "We forecast next quarter sales" (what will happen)
- Level 4: "We recommend pricing changes" (what to do)
- Level 5: "System automatically adjusts pricing" (self-optimizing)

**Diagnostic Question:**
"Where is YOUR organization's data maturity? Most say Level 2-3. That means Level 5 AI initiatives will struggle."

**Part B: Three Horizons Model (15 min)**

**Core Concept:**
Draw horizons on flipchart:
```
Horizon 1: Extend & Defend Core (60-70%)
Horizon 2: Build Emerging Capabilities (20-30%)
Horizon 3: Create Transformational Options (10-15%)
```

**Key Message:**
"You need a portfolio of AI investments across time horizons. All H1 = no future. All H3 = no present. Balance matters."

**Investment Guidance:**
- **H1**: Quick ROI (200%+ Year 1), low risk, core business
- **H2**: Medium ROI (150%+ over 3 years), medium risk, adjacent opportunities
- **H3**: Long ROI (100%+ over 5+ years), high risk, transformational

**Ask Participants:**
"Think about your AI portfolio. What % is in each horizon? Most organizations are 90% H1, 10% H2, 0% H3. That's short-term thinking."

**Transition:**
"Now let's apply this framework to competitive strategy..."

### 1:15-1:45 PM: Framework 3 - AI Investment Model

**This is Critical - Sets Up Exercise 3**

**Core Concept (10 min):**
"Move beyond 'AI for AI's sake.' Quantify business value across FOUR categories:"

1. **Cost Reduction**: Save money (labor, materials, waste)
2. **Revenue Growth**: Make more money (sales, retention, pricing)
3. **Risk Reduction**: Avoid losses (fraud, compliance, errors)
4. **Strategic Positioning**: Build capabilities (competitive advantage, innovation platform)

"First three are quantifiable. Fourth is strategic judgment. All matter."

**RetailFlow Example (15 min):**

**Introduce RetailFlow:**
"RetailFlow is a $500M omnichannel retailer. CEO has $2M AI budget. Four competing initiatives. How should he decide?"

**Walk Through ONE Example (Chatbot):**

*Project on screen or flipchart:*
- **Cost**: $450K (upfront + Year 1)
- **Cost Reduction**: Handle 72,000 inquiries automatically → save $864K/year
- **Revenue Growth**: Faster response → reduce cart abandonment → +$250K
- **Risk Reduction**: Consistent answers → reduce compliance issues
- **Strategic Positioning**: Foundation for AI customer experience
- **ROI**: 92% Year 1, 200%+ over 3 years
- **Horizon**: H1 (optimize operations)
- **Matrix Position**: Optimize quadrant

**Key Message:**
"This framework forces you to think rigorously. Not 'this seems cool' but 'this delivers $X value in Y timeframe.'"

**Introduce Exercise 3 (5 min):**
"You'll pitch and evaluate FOUR RetailFlow AI initiatives. Investment Committee has $2M to allocate. Not everything can be funded. You'll use this framework to decide."

**Form Teams:**
- 3-4 "Project Teams" (assign 1 scenario each)
- 1 "Investment Committee" (strategic role)

"Project Teams: Prepare 10-min pitch. Investment Committee: Prepare tough questions. You have 30 minutes to prepare..."

### 1:45-3:15 PM: EXERCISE 3 - AI INVESTMENT DRAGON'S DEN ⭐

**THIS IS THE CORE LEARNING ACTIVITY - MANAGE IT CAREFULLY**

**Setup (10 min):**
1. Form teams (see assignment strategy below)
2. Project teams to separate areas with scenario handouts + calculator templates
3. Investment Committee stays at main table with all 4 scenarios
4. Distribute materials

**Team Assignment Strategy:**

*Option A: Pre-assign roles* (recommended if time-constrained)
- Assign based on participant backgrounds
- Mix experience levels in each project team
- Put most senior/strategic thinkers on Investment Committee

*Option B: Self-select*
- Let participants choose (may create imbalance)
- Faster but less controlled

**Instructions to Project Teams (2 min):**
"You have 30 minutes to prepare a 10-minute pitch:
1. Use the Investment Calculator template
2. Calculate ROI for your initiative
3. Position on AI Transformation Matrix and Three Horizons
4. Identify risks and mitigation
5. Build compelling business case

You're competing for limited budget. Make it count."

**Instructions to Investment Committee (2 min):**
"You're the executive team. $2M budget. Four proposals. Your job:
1. Review all four scenarios (30 min prep)
2. Ask tough questions during pitches
3. Deliberate using strategic frameworks
4. Allocate budget by end of exercise

You can fund 0, 1, 2, 3, or all 4 initiatives - as long as total ≤ $2M. Consider portfolio balance."

**Preparation Phase (30 min):**

**Your Role:**
- **Circulate among Project Teams** - ensure they're using calculator, not just winging it
- **Help with calculations** - but don't do it for them
- **Push strategic thinking** - "Why is this a priority vs. other options?"
- **Coach Investment Committee** - "What criteria will you use? How will you balance portfolio?"
- **Manage time** - announce at 15 min and 25 min marks

**Pitches & Deliberation (45 min):**

**Format (15 min per team × 3 teams = 45 min):**
- Pitch: 7-8 minutes
- Q&A from Committee: 5-6 minutes
- Committee huddle (brief): 2 minutes

**Your Role as Facilitator:**
- **Timekeeper** - STRICTLY enforce time limits
- **Observer** - note good arguments and weak spots
- **Questioner** (if Committee needs help): Ask probing questions:
  - "How does this compare to other initiatives on ROI?"
  - "Which horizon is this, and do we have the right balance?"
  - "What happens if adoption is slower than expected?"
  - "Does this build capabilities for future initiatives?"

**Committee Decision (10 min):**
"Investment Committee, please deliberate and announce your investment decisions. Remember: $2M budget, consider portfolio balance, justify your choices."

**Typical Outcomes:**
- **Conservative**: Fund low-risk H1 initiatives (Chatbot + Fraud Detection)
- **Balanced**: Mix H1 + H2 (Chatbot + Dynamic Pricing)
- **Aggressive**: High-impact operational (Inventory + Fraud Detection)
- **Strategic**: Long-term capability building (Dynamic Pricing + Inventory)

**Debrief (10 min):**

**To Investment Committee:**
"Walk us through your decision. Why these initiatives? Why not the others?"

**Key Questions to Ask Group:**
1. "What made certain pitches stronger? Was it ROI, strategic fit, or something else?"
2. "How did you balance near-term ROI vs. long-term capability building?"
3. "Were you surprised by the committee's decision?"
4. "How is this similar or different to how your organization makes AI investment decisions?"

**Insights to Surface:**
- **ROI isn't everything**: Highest ROI doesn't always win (strategic fit matters)
- **Portfolio thinking**: Committee balanced across horizons, not just maximum ROI
- **Risk matters**: High-return but high-risk initiatives need different evaluation
- **Communication matters**: Better pitch doesn't mean better initiative (but it wins budget!)

**Critical Facilitator Moves:**
- **Celebrate good thinking**: "Did you notice how Team X positioned their initiative in context of other investments? That's strategic thinking."
- **Call out biases**: "We funded two H1 initiatives. Are we being too conservative?"
- **Connect to their reality**: "How many of you have sat in meetings like this? Did today's frameworks help?"

**If Running Long:**
- Cut to 2 pitches instead of 3 (pre-select strongest scenarios)
- Reduce prep time to 20 min (but quality suffers)
- Combine Q&A and deliberation

**If Running Short:**
- Add 4th pitch
- Do deeper debrief
- Have Project Teams peer-review each other's pitches

### 3:30-4:00 PM: Framework 4 - Innovation Adoption Framework

**Energy Management:**
*Post-break, post-major-exercise = lower energy. Keep this punchy and practical.*

**Core Concept (10 min):**
"AI implementation isn't an event - it's a journey through five phases:"

Draw on flipchart:
```
Phase 1: Knowledge Building
Phase 2: Pilot Experimentation
Phase 3: Selective Deployment
Phase 4: Scaling Operations
Phase 5: Comprehensive Transformation
```

**Key Message:**
"Most AI failures happen because organizations skip phases. You can't jump from Phase 1 to Phase 5. Respect the journey."

**Each Phase Explained (15 min):**

**Phase 1: Knowledge Building**
- What: Learn about AI, explore use cases, build awareness
- Duration: 3-6 months
- Output: Strategic roadmap, use case prioritization
- Investment: $50-200K (low commitment)
- Risk: Low (you're just learning)

**Phase 2: Pilot Experimentation**
- What: Test with limited scope, prove technical feasibility
- Duration: 6-12 months
- Output: Working prototype, ROI validation
- Investment: $200K-1M
- Risk: Medium (might not work)

**Phase 3: Selective Deployment**
- What: Roll out to subset of users/locations
- Duration: 12-18 months
- Output: Scaled implementation, refined processes
- Investment: $1-5M
- Risk: Medium-High (change management challenges)

**Phase 4: Scaling Operations**
- What: Expand across organization, integrate into operations
- Duration: 18-36 months
- Output: Enterprise-wide capability
- Investment: $5-20M
- Risk: High (organizational transformation)

**Phase 5: Comprehensive Transformation**
- What: AI embedded in culture, continuous innovation
- Duration: 36+ months (ongoing)
- Output: AI-driven organization
- Investment: $20M+ (continuous)
- Risk: Very High (business model change)

**Ask Participants:**
"Where is your organization on this journey? Most say Phase 1-2. That means Phase 5 initiatives (like autonomous operations) will fail. Build the foundation first."

**Scale/Pivot/Kill Criteria (5 min):**

"At end of each phase, decide: Scale, Pivot, or Kill"

| Signal | Scale | Pivot | Kill |
|--------|-------|-------|------|
| **ROI** | Exceeding targets | Below but improving | Negative |
| **Technical** | Proven | Fixable issues | Fundamental flaws |
| **Adoption** | Users love it | Mixed feedback | Resistance |
| **Strategic Fit** | Core to strategy | Important | No longer priority |

"Be honest. Killing a failed initiative is strategic. Don't throw good money after bad."

**Transition:**
"Now let's practice rapid strategic decisions using these frameworks..."

## Facilitator Tips & Tricks

### Energy Management

**High Energy Moments:**
- Icebreaker (beginning)
- Exercise 1 debrief (morning)
- Dragon's Den pitches (afternoon peak)

**Energy Dips:**
- Post-lunch (1:15 PM) - Framework 3 lecture
- Post-break (3:30 PM) - Framework 4 lecture

**How to Manage:**
- Keep post-lunch lecture punchy with examples
- Post-break: Make Framework 4 interactive (ask for show of hands frequently)
- Use breaks strategically to reset energy

### Common Participant Questions

**Q: "Our organization isn't ready for this. We barely have data infrastructure."**
A: "That's why Data Value Pyramid matters. Start at your level. Don't try to jump to Level 5. But start moving UP the pyramid intentionally."

**Q: "This all sounds great, but our culture resists change. How do we overcome that?"**
A: "Start small (H1 initiatives with clear ROI). Build credibility. Then tackle H2/H3. Change management is critical - that's why Innovation Adoption Framework has 5 phases, not 1."

**Q: "What if we can't calculate ROI precisely? Too many variables."**
A: "Perfect precision isn't the goal. Directional accuracy is. Is it $500K or $2M? Big difference. Is it $1.8M or $2.2M? Close enough to decide. Don't let perfection paralyze you."

**Q: "Should we build, buy, or partner for AI?"**
A: "Depends on strategic importance and your capabilities. Core competitive advantage? Build. Commodity capability? Buy. Emerging tech? Partner. Use the frameworks to decide."

### What Could Go Wrong & How to Fix

**Problem: Exercise 1 takes too long (>45 min)**
- Fix: Cut debrief to 10 min, move to next section

**Problem: Dragon's Den preparation phase chaotic**
- Fix: Give clearer structure: "First 10 min = fill out calculator. Next 10 min = build pitch. Last 10 min = practice."

**Problem: Investment Committee doesn't take role seriously**
- Fix: Before prep, emphasize: "This is the most important role. You're learning to make strategic decisions. Ask tough questions. Make hard calls."

**Problem: Participants want to debate framework validity**
- Fix: "These frameworks aren't perfect - they're useful. We're practicing strategic thinking, not memorizing theories. Let's apply them and see if they help."

**Problem: One person dominates discussions**
- Fix: "Let's hear from someone who hasn't shared yet" or use structured turn-taking in exercises

**Problem: Group is ahead of schedule**
- Fix: Do ALL 4 Dragon's Den pitches, deeper debriefs, extended action planning

**Problem: Group is behind schedule**
- Fix: Cut Exercise 4 to 2 scenarios (10 min), reduce action planning (5 min)

### Making It Relevant

**Customize for Industries:**
- **Healthcare**: Use medical AI examples (diagnostics, treatment planning)
- **Manufacturing**: Use predictive maintenance, quality control examples
- **Financial Services**: Use fraud detection, robo-advisors examples
- **Retail**: RetailFlow works well as-is

**Customize for Seniority:**
- **C-Suite**: Emphasize Three Horizons portfolio balance, strategic positioning
- **Middle Management**: Emphasize ROI calculation, Scale/Pivot/Kill decisions
- **Mix**: Balance both (as designed)

### Your Key Success Factors

**1. Master the Frameworks**
You can't teach what you don't understand. Study the frameworks reference sheet thoroughly.

**2. Be Strategic, Not Technical**
Resist going deep on "how AI works." Focus on "how to decide strategically." They don't need to understand neural networks.

**3. Connect to Their Reality**
Use their icebreaker examples throughout the day: "Remember when [Name] mentioned [initiative]? Which quadrant is that?"

**4. Facilitate, Don't Lecture**
Aim for 40% you talking, 60% them working/discussing. The learning happens in exercises, not lectures.

**5. Let Dragon's Den Be Messy**
Don't over-control Exercise 3. Let teams struggle a bit. The messiness mirrors real strategic decision-making.

**6. End Strong**
Last 15 minutes (action planning + close) set the tone for application. Make it personal and actionable.

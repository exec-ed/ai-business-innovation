## Delivery Tips

### Energy Management
- **Highest energy**: Dragon's Den (1:45-3:15 PM)
- **Lowest energy**: Post-lunch (1:15 PM), Post-break (3:30 PM)
- **Strategy**: Keep lectures punchy, exercises energizing

### Timing Priorities
- **Cannot shorten**: Dragon's Den (minimum 75 min)
- **Can shorten**: Exercise 4 (to 10 min), Framework 4 (to 20 min)
- **If running long**: Skip Exercise 4 entirely, give Innovation Adoption as handout

### Critical Success Factors
1. Master the 5 frameworks before delivering
2. Let Dragon's Den be messy (that's where learning happens)
3. Connect frameworks to participants' real situations
4. Facilitate discussion, don't just lecture
5. End with strong call to action (personal action plans)

## Course Differentiators

### vs. Typical "AI for Business" Courses
- **Not technical** - Zero discussion of neural networks, algorithms
- **Decision-focused** - How to evaluate and choose, not how to build
- **Portfolio approach** - Not just "should we do AI?" but "where should we invest?"
- **Frameworks-driven** - Systematic tools, not just case studies

### vs. Consulting Workshops
- **Actionable templates** - Take-home tools they can use tomorrow
- **Peer learning** - Dragon's Den creates cross-organizational insights
- **Honest about trade-offs** - Not "AI solves everything" but "AI requires strategic choices"

## What's NOT Included (Intentionally)

### Technical Content
- How neural networks work
- Python coding
- Data science methods
- ML algorithms

**Rationale**: Executives don't need to know *how* AI works, they need to know *how to decide* about AI strategically.

### Implementation Details
- Vendor selection
- Project management
- Agile methodologies
- Technical architecture

**Rationale**: That's the Leadership-PM course. This course is strategy, not execution.

### Industry-Specific Content
- No deep-dive on any one industry
- RetailFlow is generic enough to apply broadly
- Facilitator can customize examples

**Rationale**: Keep it broadly applicable, customize during delivery.

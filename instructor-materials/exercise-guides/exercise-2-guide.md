# Exercise 2 Instructor Guide: AI-Assisted Strategic Analysis

**Duration:** 60 minutes (11:30 AM - 12:30 PM)  
**This is NEW** - First time students use AI tools hands-on

---

## Your Role

You are **facilitating AI-augmented strategic thinking**, not teaching AI prompting.

**Goals:**
1. Students experience AI as strategic thought partner (not magic, not useless)
2. Students see AI's strengths (breadth, alternatives) AND limitations (needs context)
3. Students leave wanting to use AI this afternoon (organic adoption)

**Not goals:**
- Teaching technical AI concepts
- Making everyone AI experts
- Forcing AI usage this afternoon

---

## Phase 1: Human Brainstorm (15 min) - 11:30-11:45

### Your Introduction (3 min)

**Script:**
"Exercise 2 is different. You're going to USE AI tools—ChatGPT or Claude—for strategic analysis.

Here's the structure:
- First: Human brainstorm (your expertise)
- Second: AI augmentation (see what AI adds)
- Third: Compare and learn (human + AI = better)

Why this matters: If AI can help YOU—experts with deep industry knowledge—think better about strategy, imagine what it can do for your organization when deployed thoughtfully.

The measure of success: This afternoon during Dragon's Den prep, someone says 'Let's ask Claude about our pitch' without me suggesting it. That means you experienced AI's value."

**Give instructions (2 min):**
"Phase 1 is traditional brainstorming. 15 minutes.

RED sticky notes: AI threats to your business
- How could AI disrupt you?
- What could competitors do?
- What if you DON'T invest?

GREEN sticky notes: AI opportunities  
- Where could AI create advantage?
- What problems could AI solve?

Place on Three Horizons grid. One idea per note. Go."

### Your Role During Phase 1 (10 min)

**Circulate and observe:**
- Are tables generating 10+ ideas?
- Are they placing on horizons appropriately?
- Is there good discussion or just silent note-writing?

**Facilitate if stuck:**
- "What keeps you up at night about AI competition?" (threat prompting)
- "Where do you waste the most human time that AI could help?" (opportunity prompting)
- "Is that really H3 or is someone already doing it?" (horizon calibration)

**Don't:**
- Give them ideas (defeats the purpose)
- Rush them ("faster, more notes!")
- Critique their ideas yet (that's Phase 3)

**Common patterns you'll see:**
- ❌ Most threats are H1-H2 (near-term, obvious)
- ❌ Most opportunities are H3 (aspirational, vague)
- ❌ Clustering: 8 of 10 ideas are similar
- ✅ This is expected—Phase 2 will expand their thinking

**Timing warning (at 13 min):**
"2 minutes left. Finish your last few ideas."

## Phase 2: AI Augmentation (30 min) - 11:45-12:15

**This is where the magic happens. Facilitate carefully.**

### Your Transition (2 min)

**Script:**
"Okay, stop. You've generated [X] ideas from your collective expertise. That's valuable.

Now we're going to use AI in three ways to challenge your thinking:
1. Risk Deep-Dive: AI identifies threats you missed (10 min)
2. Reverse Prompting: AI asks YOU questions to reveal opportunities (10 min)
3. AI Debate: Two AIs argue different perspectives (10 min)

Each table: One person is 'AI Operator' (types), one is 'Scribe' (captures insights), everyone participates.

Open ChatGPT or Claude now. Operator, you're going to copy prompts from your Strategic Prompting Guide."

**If technical issues:**
"Tables without AI access: Watch my screen, we'll do this together." [Use your laptop + projector]

### Round 1: Risk Deep-Dive (10 min) - 11:45-11:55

**Show the prompt on slide or read aloud:**
"Turn to page 2 of your Strategic Prompting Guide. You'll see 'Risk Deep-Dive' prompt template. Customize it with YOUR threats."

**Give 2 minutes for customization, then:**
"Okay, submit your prompts. Read AI's response aloud to your table."

### Your Role During Round 1 (8 min after prompts submitted)

**Circulate rapidly. Listen for:**

**Good signs:**
- "Whoa, AI identified something we completely missed"
- "This second-order effect is interesting"
- Animated discussion about AI's suggestions

**Concerning signs:**
- Silence (not discussing AI output)
- "This is generic, not useful" (prompt was probably too vague)
- "AI doesn't understand our business" (true, but missing the point)

**Interventions:**

**If table is silent:**
"What did AI identify that you hadn't thought of? Anything surprising?"

**If "AI is too generic":**
"Try a follow-up prompt: 'Be more specific to [YOUR INDUSTRY]. Give concrete examples.'"

**If "AI doesn't get our business":**
"That's right—AI has breadth, you have depth. What can you TAKE from its breadth and combine with your context?"

**Model good prompting if needed:**
Walk to projector, show your screen:
"Let me show you an example. I'll ask about retail." [Submit prompt, get response]. "See how it identified [X]? That's the kind of insight you're looking for. Now keep going."

**Time check (at 8 min):**
"30 seconds—capture 2-3 AI insights on sticky notes, different color."

### Round 2: Reverse Prompting (10 min) - 11:55-12:05

**Your introduction (1 min):**
"Round 2 is different. Instead of asking AI for answers, you're going to ask AI to ask YOU questions. This is powerful—AI's questions often reveal blind spots.

Turn to 'Reverse Prompting' template. Submit that prompt. AI will ask your first question."

**Give 1 minute to submit prompts, then:**
"AI should be asking you a question now. Discuss your answer as a table for 1-2 minutes, then Operator types your response. AI will ask next question based on your answer."

### Your Role During Round 2 (8 min)

**This is the most powerful technique. Facilitate well:**

**Watch for:**
- Tables having "aha!" moments ("Oh, that question reveals we're overlooking X!")
- Good dialogue (discussing answers before typing)
- Going too fast (typing quick shallow answers vs. deep discussion)

**Intervention if going too fast:**
"Don't rush. When AI asks a question, discuss it for 1-2 minutes BEFORE answering. The questions are designed to make you think."

**Intervention if stuck:**
"What did that question make you realize? What opportunity is hiding in your answer?"

**Model good engagement if needed:**
[On projector] "Watch this. AI asked me: 'What manual process takes most time?' I'm going to answer honestly..." [Type thoughtful answer]. "Now AI's next question builds on my answer. See how this is a dialogue?"

**Common "aha!" moments you'll hear:**
- "We've never questioned why we do [X] manually"
- "That process we take for granted could be completely reinvented"
- "The question is more valuable than the answer"

**Celebrate these:**
"That's exactly the insight. AI's questions revealed an opportunity hiding in plain sight."

**Time check (at 8 min):**
"Last question. After AI responds, capture the opportunities this revealed."

### Round 3: AI Debate (10 min) - 12:05-12:15

**Your introduction (1 min):**
"Final round: AI Debate. You're going to have two AIs argue different positions:
- AI Optimist: 'Invest in AI aggressively NOW'  
- AI Pragmatist: 'Build data infrastructure FIRST'

This shows you how AI can explore multiple perspectives. It's fascinating to watch."

**Options:**

**Option A (if tables have tech skills):**
"Open two browser tabs—ChatGPT in one, Claude in another. Use the debate prompts from your guide. Let them argue."

**Option B (simpler):**
"I'll demonstrate on the projector. Watch the AIs debate, then we'll discuss."

**Recommended: Option B (demonstrate yourself)**
- Faster (no setup time per table)
- Everyone sees same debate (easier to discuss)
- You control pacing

### Running the AI Debate (8 min)

**On projector:**

1. Open ChatGPT: Submit "AI Optimist" prompt (use retail/their industry as example)
2. Read opening argument aloud: "ChatGPT says: '[READ IT]'"
3. Open Claude (or new ChatGPT conversation): Submit "AI Pragmatist" prompt
4. Read opening argument aloud: "Claude says: '[READ IT]'"
5. Copy Optimist argument → Paste into Pragmatist: "Respond to this"
6. Read Pragmatist's counter aloud
7. Copy Pragmatist counter → Paste into Optimist: "Counter this"
8. Let them debate 2-3 rounds

**Facilitate the watching:**
"Show of hands: Who finds AI Optimist more convincing?"  
[Some hands]

"Who finds AI Pragmatist more convincing?"  
[Some hands]

"Why? What made that argument stronger?"

**Key teaching points:**
- Different AIs have different "thinking styles"
- Best answer is usually synthesis ("Quick wins NOW, build foundation for H2/H3")
- This technique helps clarify your OWN thinking by seeing extremes debated

**Time check:**
"We're moving to Phase 3—comparing human vs. AI insights."

---

## Phase 3: Human Synthesis (15 min) - 12:15-12:30

**This is where learning is consolidated. Don't rush this.**

### Your Framing (2 min)

**Script:**
"Okay, you've used AI three different ways. Now comes the key question: What did you learn?

Not 'What did AI say?'—but 'How did AI change your strategic thinking?'

Discuss at your tables for 5 minutes:
1. What did AI identify that humans missed?
2. What did humans see that AI missed?
3. Which AI insights are most valuable?
4. How would you use AI for strategic decisions going forward?"

### Your Role During Table Discussions (5 min)

**Circulate and listen. Seed key points if not emerging:**

**If tables focus only on AI's value:**
"What did AI miss? What context did you have that AI didn't?"  
[They'll realize: Industry nuance, political context, gut instinct about competitors]

**If tables dismiss AI as unhelpful:**
"Did AI identify ANY threat or opportunity you hadn't considered?"  
[Almost always yes—help them see that one insight has value]

**If tables debate whether AI was "right":**
"AI isn't right or wrong—it's a thought partner. Did it make you THINK differently? That's the value."

**Key themes to listen for (capture on flipchart):**
- "AI has breadth, we have depth"
- "AI challenges assumptions we didn't know we had"
- "AI asks questions we should ask ourselves"
- "AI helps, but humans still decide"
- "Prompt engineering matters—better prompts = better insights"

### Group Shareout (8 min) - 12:23-12:30

**Ask for volunteers (or call on tables):**
"Each table: Share ONE AI-generated insight that changed your thinking. What did AI help you see?"

**As each table shares, capture themes:**
- What patterns do you see? (AI often identifies threats from adjacent industries)
- What surprises emerge? (Reverse prompting reveals the most "hidden" opportunities)
- Any skepticism? (Healthy—use it: "Yes, AI isn't magic. It's augmentation.")

### Your Closing (2 min) - 12:28-12:30

**Script:**
"What you experienced this morning:
- AI as strategic thought partner, not magic oracle
- AI's strengths: Breadth, alternatives, challenging assumptions, asking questions
- AI's limitations: Needs YOUR context, can't replace YOUR judgment
- Best results: Human expertise + AI analysis

This afternoon:
- Dragon's Den prep: 1:45-2:30 PM
- AI tools available if you choose to use them
- We won't mandate it

The measure of success: Someone says 'Let's ask Claude about our pitch' without me suggesting it. That means this morning worked—you experienced AI's value and naturally reach for it.

Lunch break. See you at 1:15 PM."

## Common Facilitator Challenges

### Challenge 1: "AI gave generic responses"

**Cause:** Prompt lacked context or was too vague

**Fix:**
"Try adding more context. Tell AI: 'Our industry is [X], our specific challenge is [Y], be specific.'"

**Teaching moment:**
"This is why prompt engineering matters. AI is only as good as your prompt. Better context → Better insights."

### Challenge 2: "Our table doesn't have internet"

**Fix (during exercise):**
"Watch my screen—we'll do this together." [Demonstrate on projector]

**After exercise:**
"Even watching AI used well teaches you how to prompt. You can practice on your own later."

### Challenge 3: "This feels forced/artificial"

**Empathize:**
"You're right, it's not how you normally work. But humor me for 30 minutes."

**After Phase 2:**
"Now that you've tried it—did AI add ANY value? One insight you didn't have?"  
[Almost always yes]  
"That's why it's worth the awkwardness. One good insight makes it worthwhile."

### Challenge 4: "We went too fast/too slow"

**Too fast (done with Phase 2 in 20 min):**
- Add 4th technique: "AI as Devil's Advocate—challenge your best idea"
- Go deeper on one technique: "Ask AI 5 follow-up questions on that risk"

**Too slow (still in Phase 2 at 12:10):**
- Skip AI Debate, you summarize the key arguments
- Or show pre-prepared debate example (save 8 minutes)

### Challenge 5: "Students want to use AI for everything now"

**This is actually success—but needs calibration:**
"AI helps you THINK. Frameworks help you DECIDE. You need both.

This afternoon: AI can help generate ideas, identify risks, challenge assumptions. But YOU still make the investment decision using the AI Investment Checklist. AI informs, you decide."

### Challenge 6: "AI said something wrong/misleading"

**Perfect teaching moment:**
"Great catch! That's why you can't accept AI output blindly. AI makes confident mistakes.

This is why you need YOUR expertise to evaluate AI's suggestions. AI generates, you validate."

## Backup Plans

### If internet fails completely

**Pivot to discussion:**
"Internet's out. Let me tell you what AI WOULD have said..." [Use answer-keys/exercise-2-ai-responses.md]

"Now discuss: Would that have changed your thinking? How?"

**You still teach:**
- AI as thought partner concept
- Prompt engineering importance
- Human + AI collaboration

**Missing:** Hands-on experience (but learning still happens)

### If only 2 tables have AI access

**Make them "demo tables":**
"These two tables will use AI, everyone else watch their screens and discuss."

"After 10 minutes, demo tables share what AI said."

**Hybrid approach works fine.**

### If students resist ("This is gimmicky")

**Acknowledge:**
"I get it—feels forced. Bear with me for 20 minutes."

**After Phase 2:**
"Okay, honest assessment: Did AI add value? Did you learn anything about how to use AI for strategy?"

**Usually converts skeptics when they see results.**

## Post-Exercise Actions

**Immediately after (12:30):**
- Note which tables had great AI interactions (their prompts were good)
- Note which tables struggled (tech issues? poor prompts? skepticism?)
- Adjust afternoon accordingly (more or less emphasis on AI availability)

**During lunch:**
- Be available for questions about AI tools
- If someone asks detailed AI questions: "Great question—let's discuss after lunch so others can eat"

**This afternoon:**
- Don't force AI usage, but have Strategic Prompting Guide visible
- If no one uses AI by 2:00 PM: "Remember, AI tools are available if you want to stress-test your thinking" (light nudge, not mandate)
- If someone uses AI: Briefly acknowledge: "Great—using the tools from this morning"

## Debrief Integration (4:10-4:25 PM)

**When debriefing Dragon's Den, connect to Exercise 2:**

"This morning you used AI for strategic analysis. Did anyone use AI this afternoon?"

**If yes:**
"How did it help? What insights did it generate?"  
[Validates the technique]

**If no:**
"That's okay—you now know AI is available as a thought partner when you need it. The goal wasn't forcing usage, it was experiencing the value."

**Either way:**
"What did this morning's AI exercise teach you about AI as augmentation vs. replacement?"

**Key learning to emphasize:**
- AI helped WITH strategic thinking (breadth, alternatives, questions)
- But humans still used FRAMEWORKS to decide (Three Horizons, Investment Checklist)
- Best results: Human judgment + AI analysis
- This IS the future of strategic work

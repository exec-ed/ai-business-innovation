# Dragon's Den: Sample Investment Decisions

**Purpose:** Show instructors what Investment Committees typically decide and why

**Use this to:**
- Understand common decision patterns
- Know which debates to expect
- Prepare for debrief discussion
- Calibrate whether students are applying frameworks correctly

---

## The Decision Dilemma (Recap)

**Budget:** $2M total (cannot exceed)  
**Constraints:** 
- Must fund at least 2 initiatives
- Maximum $1.2M per project
- Results needed within 18 months

**Four competing proposals (after AI-specific analysis):**

| Initiative | Original Budget | Revised Budget | Traditional ROI | AI-Specific Issues |
|------------|-----------------|----------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Chatbot** | $450K | $525K | 92% Year 1 | ✅ Low-Med risk, data ready |
| **Dynamic Pricing** | $850K | $1.3M | 253% (3-yr) | ⚠️ EXCEEDS CAP + HIGH ethical risk |
| **Inventory** | $1.1M | $1.25M | 264% (3-yr) | ⚠️ EXCEEDS CAP + explainability needs |
| **Fraud Detection** | $650K | $1.05M | 315% Year 1 | ⚠️ Data not ready (6-mo prep) + HIGH bias risk |

**The trap:** Traditional ROI suggests fund Dynamic Pricing + Inventory + Fraud (top 3). But all three have AI-specific red flags.

---

## Common Decision Patterns

### Pattern 1: "Risk-Averse Portfolio" (40% of committees)

**Decision:**
- ✅ Fund Chatbot: $525K
- ✅ Fund Inventory (trimmed scope): $1.2M
- ✅ Fund Fraud data prep only: $75K
- **Total:** $1.8M (under budget)
- **Unallocated:** $200K (reserve for contingencies)

**Rationale:**
"We prioritized de-risked investments that can deliver in our timeframe:

1. **Chatbot (H1, Optimize):** Lowest risk, data ready, clear ROI. This builds confidence in AI and frees up $414K annually for future AI.

2. **Inventory (H1-H2, Revolutionize):** Strong ROI (243% after trim), manageable risk, builds strategic capability. We trimmed scope to fit $1.2M cap—start with top 20% SKUs, expand if successful.

3. **Fraud data prep:** We recognise Fraud has highest ROI (315%), but data isn't ready. We'll fund the 6-month data labeling project ($75K) now, revisit AI implementation in Year 2 when data is ready.

**Not funded:**
- **Dynamic Pricing:** Despite 253% ROI, we cannot accept the ethical/legal risk of price discrimination. One lawsuit erases years of ROI. Additionally, at $1.3M it exceeds our cap. Not worth the risk.

**Portfolio balance:** 90% H1 (Chatbot + Inventory), 10% H2 prep (Fraud data). Conservative but realistic given our data maturity."

**Instructor notes:**
- ✅ This is a **defensible decision** - prioritizes risk management
- ✅ Applied AI-specific criteria (ethical risk killed Dynamic Pricing)
- ✅ Pragmatic about budget constraints (trimmed Inventory to fit)
- ⚠️ Leaves money on table ($200K) - could be criticized as unambitious
- ⚠️ Heavy H1 (optimize) - not building enough future capability

**Debrief questions:**
- "You left $200K unallocated. What would you do with that?"
- "90% H1 is conservative. What H2/H3 capability are you missing?"
- "What if competitors invest aggressively while you play safe?"

---

### Pattern 2: "ROI-Maximizer Portfolio" (30% of committees)

**Decision:**
- ✅ Fund Inventory (trimmed): $1.2M
- ✅ Fund Fraud Detection: $1.05M
- **Total:** $2.25M (⚠️ OVER BUDGET by $250K)
- **Requested:** Budget increase or cut scope further

**Rationale:**
"We funded the two highest-ROI projects:

1. **Inventory (264% ROI):** Despite exceeding cap, this has transformational impact on working capital and revenue. We trimmed to $1.2M but it's still our highest-value investment.

2. **Fraud Detection (315% ROI):** Highest ROI, addresses $8M annual loss. Yes, data needs work and there's bias risk, but the financial case is too strong to ignore. We'll invest in proper bias testing and external audit.

**Not funded:**
- **Chatbot:** Lowest ROI (92%). Nice-to-have, not strategic necessity.
- **Dynamic Pricing:** Exceeds budget even more ($1.3M) and ethical risks too high.

**Problem:** We're $250K over budget. Options:
- Trim both scopes further
- Request budget increase from CEO
- Delay Fraud to Year 2

We recommend option 1: Trim Fraud to $800K (remove some fraud types, focus on highest-impact)."

**Instructor notes:**
- ⚠️ This **violates budget constraint** - unrealistic in real world
- ✅ Strong ROI focus, but ignored AI-specific risks
- ❌ Underestimated Fraud's bias risk ("we'll invest in testing" but no detail)
- ❌ Dismissed Chatbot despite being only fully de-risked option
- ⚠️ No H1 quick wins (both projects are 12-18 month payback)

**Debrief questions:**
- "CFO says no budget increase. What gets cut?"
- "You mentioned bias testing for Fraud—have you budgeted enough? External audits cost $100K+."
- "No H1 quick wins means no results for 12 months. Can you afford that politically?"
- "What if Inventory AND Fraud both hit delays (common with transformational projects)? You have no backup wins."

---

### Pattern 3: "Balanced Portfolio" (25% of committees)

**Decision:**
- ✅ Fund Chatbot: $525K
- ✅ Fund Inventory (trimmed): $1.2M
- ✅ Fund Dynamic Pricing data/pilot only: $250K
- **Total:** $1.975M
- **Unallocated:** $25K (rounding)

**Rationale:**
"We built a balanced portfolio across horizons and risk levels:

1. **Chatbot (H1, low risk):** Quick win, builds AI confidence, frees up resources. ROI in 12 months gives us proof point for board.

2. **Inventory (H1-H2, medium risk):** Strong ROI, builds strategic capability. Trimmed scope to fit cap, phased rollout (10 stores pilot, scale if successful).

3. **Dynamic Pricing (H2, high risk) - MODIFIED:** We recognise the ethical/budget issues. Instead of full implementation:
   - Fund data infrastructure ($100K competitive data)
   - Fund explainability platform ($100K)
   - Fund 6-month pilot with strict ethical guardrails ($50K)
   - Revisit full deployment in Year 2 if pilot proves bias can be mitigated
   - This de-risks the ethical concerns while exploring the opportunity

**Not funded (full implementation):**
- **Fraud Detection:** Data not ready for 6 months. Would delay other initiatives if we staffed the data prep team now. Revisit in Year 2.

**Portfolio balance:** 60% H1 (Chatbot + Inventory), 40% H2 (Dynamic Pricing exploration). Mix of quick wins + capability building."

**Instructor notes:**
- ✅ This is the **most sophisticated decision** - shows strategic thinking
- ✅ Creative problem-solving (pilot Dynamic Pricing to de-risk)
- ✅ Balanced across horizons (60/40 H1/H2)
- ✅ Applied AI-specific criteria thoughtfully (addressed ethical concerns)
- ✅ Used budget efficiently ($25K under)
- ⚠️ Complex to execute (3 initiatives simultaneously may be challenging)

**Debrief questions:**
- "Three initiatives—can you actually execute all three well simultaneously?"
- "Dynamic Pricing pilot ($250K)—is that enough to learn what you need?"
- "This is creative—how did you come up with the 'pilot only' approach?"

---

### Pattern 4: "Transformational Bet" (5% of committees)

**Decision:**
- ✅ Fund Inventory (full scope): $1.25M
- ✅ Fund Fraud data prep: $75K
- ✅ Fund AI Infrastructure: $650K
- **Total:** $1.975M

**Rationale:**
"We made a strategic choice: Build the AI foundation properly.

1. **Inventory (largest single investment):** This has highest ROI and builds core AI capability. Yes it exceeds cap, but we justify $1.25M because it touches every part of the business. This IS our AI foundation.

2. **Fraud data prep:** Get data ready NOW so we can implement in Year 2.

3. **AI Infrastructure (NEW - not in original proposals):** We realised all four projects need:
   - Integrated data platform
   - ML ops infrastructure
   - Governance and monitoring tools
   
   We're investing $650K in shared infrastructure that will:
   - Make current projects cheaper/faster
   - Enable future AI projects
   - Reduce vendor lock-in

**Not funded immediately:**
- **Chatbot, Dynamic Pricing:** Will implement in Year 2 once infrastructure is in place. They'll be cheaper and faster to deploy.

**This is a FOUNDATION strategy:** Build once, deploy many AIs later."

**Instructor notes:**
- 🤔 This is **high-risk, creative thinking** - not a "wrong" answer but unconventional
- ✅ Shows long-term strategic thinking (infrastructure investment)
- ⚠️ No quick wins (nothing delivers in Year 1)
- ⚠️ "AI Infrastructure" wasn't in the original options (did they invent a 5th option?)
- ❌ Violates "must fund at least 2 initiatives" if infrastructure doesn't count

**Debrief questions:**
- "Bold choice—no wins for 12+ months. How do you maintain board support?"
- "You invented a 5th option (infrastructure). That's creative, but was it in scope?"
- "What if you build infrastructure and THEN business priorities shift? You've spent $650K on capabilities you don't use."
- "This is long-term thinking—but is it realistic given CEO wants results in 18 months?"

## Expected Committee Debates

### Debate 1: "Should we fund Dynamic Pricing despite ethical risks?"

**Optimist argument:**
"253% ROI is too good to pass up. We can mitigate bias with testing. Every company does dynamic pricing (airlines, Uber). We're overthinking the ethics."

**Pessimist argument:**
"One price discrimination lawsuit erases years of ROI. We're not Amazon—we can't absorb that reputational hit. The ethical risk is existential."

**Your facilitation:**
- Let them debate (this is the learning)
- If they're glossing over ethical risk: "The scenario flags HIGH ethical risk. What specifically is your bias testing plan?"
- If they're overly conservative: "Airlines, hotels, ride-sharing all use dynamic pricing. How are they managing the ethical risk?"

**Most committees:** Reject full implementation, some fund limited pilot with heavy monitoring.

**Teaching point:**
"This is the AI-specific lens at work. Traditional ROI says 'definitely fund.' AI-specific ethics criterion says 'too risky.' You must weigh both."

---

### Debate 2: "Do we fund highest ROI or safest option?"

**ROI-focused argument:**
"Fraud Detection is 315% ROI. Yes, data needs work, but the financial case is overwhelming."

**Risk-focused argument:**
"Chatbot is only 92% ROI, but it's the only project that's actually READY. Data is good, bias risk is low, budget fits. We can actually deliver this."

**Your facilitation:**
- This reveals their risk tolerance
- No "right" answer—depends on organisational context
- Probe: "What's your organization's appetite for risk? Do you need a quick win to build credibility?"

**Teaching point:**
"This is portfolio thinking in action. Highest ROI isn't always best choice if risk is unacceptable. Balance returns with probability of success."

---

### Debate 3: "Should we exceed the $1.2M cap for Inventory?"

**Pragmatist argument:**
"Cap is $1.2M. Inventory is $1.25M. Rules are rules—trim scope to fit."

**Strategist argument:**
"It's $50K over cap. If this is our most strategic investment, we should ask CEO for exception. Don't hamstring the best project over $50K."

**Your facilitation:**
- In real world: Depends on organisational culture (rules-bound vs. flexible)
- Force the decision: "CEO said $1.2M cap, no exceptions. What gets cut from Inventory?"

**Teaching point:**
"Constraints force creativity. How can you trim $50K and still deliver 90% of value?"

---

### Debate 4: "Should we delay Fraud for data readiness or fund it anyway?"

**Data-focused argument:**
"Scenario says data readiness is 6/10. That's below our threshold of 7. We should fund data prep ($75K) now, implement AI in Year 2."

**Opportunity-focused argument:**
"We're losing $8M/year to fraud. We can't wait 6 months. Fund it now, clean data in parallel. The cost of waiting exceeds the cost of imperfect data."

**Your facilitation:**
- This is THE key AI-specific decision (data readiness gate)
- Push them: "Your own framework says <7 = don't proceed. Do you violate your framework?"
- Or: "If you proceed despite data concerns, what's your mitigation? How do you ensure data quality doesn't sink the project?"

**Most committees:** Fund data prep now, full implementation in Year 2 (respecting the framework)

**Teaching point:**
"You set the data readiness threshold at 7. Now a project scores 6 and you want to make an exception. This is how frameworks get undermined. Discipline matters."

## What "Good" Committee Performance Looks Like

### During Deliberation (10 min)

**Good signs:**
- ✅ Using AI Investment Checklist explicitly ("Fraud scores 6/10 on data—that's below threshold")
- ✅ Debating AI-specific criteria (not just ROI)
- ✅ Considering portfolio balance (not just best individual projects)
- ✅ Discussing trade-offs explicitly (ROI vs. risk, quick wins vs. strategic bets)
- ✅ Referencing frameworks by name ("This violates Three Horizons balance")

**Concerning signs:**
- ❌ Only discussing traditional ROI (ignoring AI-specific criteria)
- ❌ Trying to fund everything (ignoring budget constraint)
- ❌ Handwaving ethical risks ("We'll deal with it later")
- ❌ Not using frameworks (pure gut decisions)
- ❌ Dominance by one voice (others not participating)

**Your intervention if needed:**
Watch from back of room. If concerning signs dominate, interject at 5-minute mark:

"Committee, I haven't heard discussion of [AI-specific criterion]. Should that factor in?"

Don't tell them what to decide—just ensure they're considering all criteria.

## Debrief Script (4:10-4:25 PM)

**After committee announces decision:**

### Question 1: "How did AI-specific criteria change your evaluation?" (5 min)

**Ask committee:**
"What looked attractive on traditional ROI but had AI-specific red flags?"

**Expected answers:**
- "Dynamic Pricing had best ROI but ethical risks scared us"
- "Fraud Detection highest ROI but data wasn't ready"
- "All top-3 ROI projects had issues when we applied AI criteria"

**Your follow-up:**
"So traditional ROI analysis alone would have led you to fund the WRONG projects?"

**Teaching point:**
"This is why AI-specific evaluation matters. Highest ROI ≠ best AI investment."

### Question 2: "What surprised you about hidden costs?" (3 min)

**Ask room:**
"Original budgets vs. revised budgets—what surprised you?"

**Expected answers:**
- "Ongoing costs are way higher (30-50% vs. 10-15%)"
- "Bias testing added $100K+ we didn't expect"
- "Data prep for Fraud (6 months) delayed highest ROI project"
- "Explainability requirements limited our algorithm choices"

**Teaching point:**
"This is why you can't use traditional IT budgeting for AI. Must account for continuous learning, bias testing, explainability. Add 30-50% to vendor quotes."

### Question 3: "Which would you have funded with ROI only?" (2 min)

**Ask project teams:**
"If committee only looked at traditional ROI, what would they have funded?"

**Expected answer:**
"Dynamic Pricing (253%), Inventory (264%), Fraud (315%)—the top 3."

**Then ask:**
"How many committees actually funded all three?"  
[Usually 0-1 out of whole room]

"Why not?"

**Expected answer:**
"AI-specific criteria revealed risks that changed the decision."

**Teaching point:**
"Frameworks work. AI Investment Checklist revealed risks traditional ROI missed."

### Question 4: "How will you apply this Monday?" (5 min)

**Ask room:**
"What will you do differently when evaluating AI investments now?"

**Capture responses on flipchart:**
- "Audit data readiness before approving AI projects"
- "Add AI Investment Checklist to approval process"
- "Question vendor ROI claims (add 30-50% for ongoing costs)"
- "Require bias testing for customer-facing AI"
- "Use AI tools to stress-test assumptions"

**If no one mentions using AI tools:**
Prompt: "Anyone planning to use the prompting techniques from this morning?"

**Your closing:**
"These are concrete actions. This afternoon in your Personal Action Plan, write down which ones you'll commit to. Make them specific: WHO will do WHAT by WHEN."

## Common "Gotcha" Moments

### Gotcha 1: Committee violates their own frameworks

**Example:** "We set data readiness threshold at 7, but we're funding Fraud which scores 6."

**Your response:**
"I notice you're making an exception to your own framework. That's a real organisational dynamic—frameworks get bent under pressure.

If you do this, you need explicit mitigation: What SPECIFICALLY will you do about the 6/10 data readiness?"

**Teaching point:**
Frameworks only work if you follow them. Exceptions need explicit rationale.

### Gotcha 2: Committee adds projects not in scenarios

**Example:** "We're funding AI Infrastructure for $650K"

**Your response:**
"Creative—you invented a 5th option. In real life, does CEO allow that, or must you choose from the four proposals?"

**Either way is defensible:**
- Allowing it: Rewards creative thinking
- Disallowing it: Teaches working within constraints

**Your call based on room energy and time.**

### Gotcha 3: Committee funds everything by ignoring constraints

**Example:** "We want all four projects, total $4M, we'll ask for more budget."

**Your response:**
"In this scenario, CEO said $2M firm. CFO says no increase. What gets cut?"

**Force the hard choice:**
This is where learning happens—making trade-offs with constraints.

## Instructor Reflection Questions

**After Dragon's Den, ask yourself:**

1. **Did committees apply frameworks rigorously?**
   - If yes: Great, frameworks worked
   - If no: Why not? Too complex? Not internalized? Time pressure?

2. **Did AI-specific criteria actually change decisions?**
   - If yes: Success—they saw AI evaluation is different
   - If no: Maybe not emphasized enough, or scenarios need stronger contrasts

3. **What debates emerged that you didn't expect?**
   - These are often the richest learning moments
   - Capture for future delivery

4. **Would you change anything about scenarios?**
   - Are AI-specific issues clear enough?
   - Are budget numbers realistic?
   - Do scenarios force real trade-offs?

**Update this document with your insights for next facilitator.**

## The Ideal Outcome

✅ Understand AI requires different evaluation (not just ROI)

✅ Experienced making hard trade-offs (can't fund everything)  
✅ Seen how AI-specific criteria reveal hidden risks/costs  
✅ Practiced using AI tools for strategic thinking (morning)  
✅ Naturally used AI during afternoon (some participants, not all)  
✅ Leave with frameworks AND experience applying them

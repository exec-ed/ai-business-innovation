# RetailFlow Reflection Prompts for Facilitators
**How to connect the RetailFlow case study to participants' real organisations**

---

## Purpose

These reflection prompts prevent the course from feeling "too academic" by regularly bridging the RetailFlow case study to participants' real organisational challenges. Use these at key transition points throughout the day.

---

## Opening: Setting Expectations (9:00-9:15 AM)

### During Welcome

**After explaining the case study approach, say:**

> "Today we'll use RetailFlow Inc. as our case study for all exercises. This isn't because your companies aren't interesting—it's because RetailFlow gives us a **safe space** to practice frameworks without worrying about IP, NDAs, or company politics.
>
> But here's the key: After each RetailFlow exercise, we'll pause and ask, **'How does this compare to YOUR experience?'** You'll constantly be translating RetailFlow's challenges to your own context.
>
> By the end of the day, you'll have practiced on RetailFlow AND created a Personal Action Plan for your own organisation. Best of both worlds."

---

## After Exercise 1: AI Tech Radar (10:25 AM)

### Debrief + Reality Check (5 minutes)

**After reviewing RetailFlow's Tech Radar results, facilitate this discussion:**

> "We just mapped RetailFlow's AI initiatives and found they're over-invested in process optimization and have low data readiness scores in several areas.
>
> **Quick reflection: Turn to your neighbour and discuss for 90 seconds:**
>
> 1. Where would YOUR organisation appear on the Data Value Pyramid? Level 2? 3? 4?
> 2. Does your company have a similar over-investment in one quadrant?
> 3. What's YOUR biggest data readiness gap?"

**Debrief to full group (2 min):**
- "Show of hands: How many of you are at Level 2-3 on data maturity?" (Most hands go up)
- "How many are trying to do Level 4-5 AI projects?" (Most same hands stay up)
- "That's the data readiness paradox RetailFlow faces—and most of you do too."

**Key transition statement:**
> "RetailFlow discovered that 3 out of 4 AI initiatives have data readiness scores below 7. They need data infrastructure work before AI investment. How many of you just realised the same thing about your organization?"

---

## After Data Value Pyramid Presentation (11:30 AM - Before Exercise 2)

### Quick Poll + Discussion (3 minutes)

**After presenting the 5 levels, ask:**

> "Based on what you just learned, where would you honestly place your organisation on the Data Value Pyramid?"
>
> **Show of hands:**
> - Level 1 (Collection): Who's here? [Few hands]
> - Level 2 (Integration): Who's here? [Many hands - capture this]
> - Level 3 (Descriptive): Who's here? [Some hands]
> - Level 4 (Predictive): Who's here? [Few hands]
> - Level 5 (Autonomous): Who's here? [Maybe zero hands]

**Key insight to emphasize:**
> "Most of you are Level 2-3, just like RetailFlow. But RetailFlow is being asked to do Level 4-5 projects—dynamic pricing, fraud detection. Sound familiar?
>
> This is why data readiness scoring matters. It forces honest conversations about whether you're ready for the AI you're being asked to build."

---

## After Exercise 2: AI-Assisted Strategic Analysis (12:30 PM - Before Lunch)

### Group Sharing + Comparison (5 minutes)

**After groups complete their competitive analysis, facilitate:**

> "RetailFlow just identified threats from three directions: online-first retailers, big box chains, and traditional competitors waking up to AI.
>
> **Let's compare:** I want to hear from 3 people about YOUR competitive landscape:
> 1. Who faces threats from **AI-native startups** entering your industry?
> 2. Who faces threats from **big tech** (Google, Amazon, Microsoft) entering your space?
> 3. Who faces threats from **traditional competitors** suddenly investing heavily in AI?

**Facilitate 3 quick responses (30-45 seconds each), then:**

> "Notice how RetailFlow's challenges—being squeezed from multiple directions—mirror what many of you face. The frameworks we're using work regardless of your industry because the **strategic logic of AI competition** is universal.
>
> RetailFlow needs to decide: Which threats do we defend against first? Which opportunities do we pursue? That's exactly what you'll be deciding for your own company."

---

## After AI Investment Model Presentation (1:45 PM - Before Dragon's Den Prep)

### Reality Check Discussion (5 minutes)

**After presenting the 5 AI-specific criteria, ask:**

> "We just walked through 5 AI-specific evaluation criteria that RetailFlow will use to evaluate projects. Let's get honest about your organisations:
>
> **Quick show of hands:**
> 1. How many of you currently evaluate AI projects using ALL 5 of these criteria? [Few/no hands]
> 2. How many evaluate AI projects the SAME WAY you evaluate traditional IT projects? [Many hands]
> 3. How many have had an AI project fail because you DIDN'T consider these criteria? [Several hands]

**Pick 1-2 people who raised their hand for #3:**
> "Can you share what happened? Which criterion did you miss?"
>
> [Listen for: data quality issues, ongoing costs higher than expected, bias discovered post-launch, etc.]

**Transition to Dragon's Den:**
> "RetailFlow is about to make the same mistakes you've learned from—unless the Investment Committee uses these criteria ruthlessly. In the Dragon's Den, you'll see how these AI-specific criteria completely change which projects look attractive.
>
> As you watch RetailFlow's investment decisions, ask yourself: **'Would my executive team catch these red flags? Or would we fund the wrong projects based on ROI alone?'**"

---

## During Dragon's Den Team Prep (1:50-2:25 PM)

### Circulate and Connect to Their Reality

**As you move between teams prepping their pitches, ask individuals:**

**To Investment Committee:**
> "As you review these proposals, which one most closely resembles an AI project YOUR company is evaluating right now?"
>
> "When you get back to work, will your CFO ask the same tough questions you're preparing to ask RetailFlow's project teams?"

**To Project Teams:**
> "As you're preparing this RetailFlow pitch, have you pitched something similar at your own company? How did it go?"
>
> "What pushback did you get from YOUR executive team that you're not anticipating for RetailFlow?"

---

## After Dragon's Den Pitches (4:10 PM - Debrief)

### The Critical Comparison Discussion (15 minutes)

**After Investment Committee announces their decision, facilitate:**

### Part 1: The Decision Comparison (5 min)

> "The Investment Committee just allocated RetailFlow's $2M. They chose [X, Y, Z projects].
>
> **Discussion questions for everyone:**
>
> 1. **If this were YOUR company**, would your executive team have made the same decision? Why or why not?
> 2. **Show of hands**: Who thinks their CEO would have overruled the committee and funded the highest-ROI project regardless of AI risks? [Many hands - discuss]
> 3. **For the committee**: What was the HARDEST part of this decision? Was it the same challenges you face in real investment decisions?"

### Part 2: The Criteria That Changed Everything (5 min)

> "Let's look at what WOULD have happened without AI-specific criteria:
>
> **Traditional ROI ranking:**
> 1. Fraud Detection: 315% ROI → Would have been funded first
> 2. Inventory: 264% ROI → Would have been funded second
> 3. Dynamic Pricing: 253% ROI → Would have been funded third
> 4. Chatbot: 92% ROI → Would have been rejected
>
> **But AI-specific criteria revealed:**
> - Fraud Detection: Data not ready (6-month delay needed)
> - Dynamic Pricing: Over budget + HIGH ethical risk (price discrimination lawsuits)
> - Inventory: Over budget (would need scope reduction)
> - Chatbot: LOW risk, data ready, fits budget → Actually the safest choice
>
> **Ask the room:**
> 1. How many of YOUR companies would have funded based on traditional ROI alone? [Most hands]
> 2. How many have made that mistake and paid for it later? [Some hands - get stories]
> 3. What's ONE AI-specific criterion you're going to add to YOUR evaluation process Monday morning?"

### Part 3: The Politics Question (5 min)

> "Let's talk about organisational reality. RetailFlow is fictional, so we could make rational decisions without politics.
>
> **But in YOUR organisations:**
> - What happens when the CEO's pet project scores low on data readiness?
> - What happens when the highest-ROI project has high ethical risk?
> - How do you say 'no' to a project with 315% ROI because the data isn't ready?
>
> **I want to hear from 2-3 people:** What's the POLITICAL challenge of using AI-specific criteria that we didn't face with RetailFlow?"

[This question reveals the messy reality of organisational decision-making and validates why practicing on RetailFlow first was valuable]

---

## Exercise 5: Personal Action Plan Introduction (4:25-4:30 PM)

### Option A: If Running On Time (5-minute Quick Start)

> "We've spent the day practicing frameworks on RetailFlow—a safe, fictional company where we could make mistakes without consequences.
>
> **Now it's time to translate these lessons to YOUR organization.**
>
> You have your Personal Action Plan worksheet. This is designed as a **take-home exercise**—it needs 45-60 minutes of thoughtful work when you have access to your organisational data and can think without exhaustion.
>
> **But let's get you started with 5 quick wins right now:**
>
> Open to the 'OPTIONAL: In-Class Quick Start' section. You have 5 minutes to:
> 1. **Capture your top insight** from RetailFlow (1 min)
> 2. **Identify your #1 AI decision** you need to evaluate (1 min)
> 3. **Flag your biggest gap** – data? portfolio? evaluation rigor? (2 min)
> 4. **Set calendar reminders** on your phone RIGHT NOW to complete this within 7 days (1 min)
>
> This quick start ensures you capture today's insights while they're fresh. Then complete the full worksheet this week."

**[Give them 5 minutes of quiet time]**

**After 5 minutes:**

> "Hands up if you set your calendar reminder?" [Most hands should go up]
>
> "Good. That's your commitment to translating RetailFlow to reality. Complete the full worksheet within 7 days—that's when you'll map your data maturity, assess your portfolio balance, evaluate a real AI project, and plan your Monday actions.
>
> **Remember**: You practiced on RetailFlow. Now apply it to YOUR decisions."

---

### Option B: If Running Behind Schedule (2-minute Send-Off)

> "You have your Personal Action Plan worksheet. This is designed as a **take-home exercise**—it needs 45-60 minutes of thoughtful work.
>
> **Right now, do ONE thing:**
>
> Pull out your phone and set a calendar reminder for 3 days from now: 'Complete AI Action Plan.'
>
> [Pause while they do this]
>
> **Complete the full worksheet this week while today's frameworks are fresh.** It will walk you through:
> - Mapping YOUR AI maturity
> - Assessing YOUR portfolio balance
> - Evaluating YOUR #1 AI investment using today's frameworks
> - Planning YOUR Monday actions
>
> This is where RetailFlow practice becomes real-world impact."

---

## Closing Remarks (4:50 PM)

### Final Reality Check

> "Today we practiced on RetailFlow. Tomorrow you go back to your organisations where:
> - The CEO has a favorite AI project
> - The CFO demands ROI certainty you can't provide
> - The data team says 'our data is fine' (it's not)
> - The vendor promises 95% accuracy (they can't guarantee it)
> - Everyone is afraid of being left behind by AI competitors
>
> **RetailFlow taught us that AI investment requires DIFFERENT evaluation:**
> - Traditional criteria are necessary but not sufficient
> - Data readiness gates everything else
> - Ongoing costs matter as much as initial investment
> - Ethical risks can be existential
> - Sometimes the 'worst' ROI project is the smartest choice
>
> **Your job now**: Bring this rigor to YOUR investment decisions. Use the frameworks. Ask the hard questions. Say 'no' to projects that fail AI-specific criteria, even if they have great traditional ROI.
>
> **Because RetailFlow is fictional, but your reputation, budget, and strategic position are real.**"

## Facilitation Tips

### Timing These Reflections

- **Keep them tight**: 3-5 minutes each
- **Use questions, not lectures**: Get participants talking to each other
- **Capture themes**: Listen for patterns and call them out
- **Don't force sharing**: Some people won't want to reveal company details—that's OK

### When Participants Resist the Case Study Approach

**If someone says: "This is too academic" or "RetailFlow isn't like my company":**

Response:
> "You're right—RetailFlow is simpler than your reality. But that's the point. If you can't apply these frameworks to a clean case study, you definitely can't apply them to your messy reality. We're building the muscle memory here so you can use it when the stakes are real."

**If someone says: "My company would never use these criteria":**

Response:
> "Maybe not today. But how many AI projects has your company funded that failed? How much of that $30-40 billion in wasted GenAI investment is yours? These criteria exist because traditional evaluation ISN'T WORKING for AI. Your job is to bring this back and start the conversation."

### Reading the Room

**If energy is low after lunch:**
- Speed up the reflection discussions (3 min instead of 5)
- Use more physical movement (stand and find a partner, move to different tables)
- Make the Dragon's Den more theatrical and high-energy

**If participants want MORE personal application:**
- Extend Exercise 5 (Personal Action Plan) to 30 minutes
- Cut 10 minutes from Dragon's Den debrief
- Offer to stay after 4:30 for 1-on-1 discussions

**If participants are defensive about their organisations:**
- Normalize the challenges: "Everyone struggles with this"
- Share anonymized examples from other courses
- Frame it as industry-wide problem, not their company's failure

---

## Summary: The Reflection Arc

The day follows a deliberate arc:

1. **Morning**: "Let's practice on RetailFlow (safe)"
2. **Midday**: "How does RetailFlow compare to your experience?" (bridging)
3. **Afternoon**: "Apply RetailFlow lessons to YOUR decisions" (transfer)
4. **Close**: "You're ready to bring this rigor to your organization" (action)

By building in these reflection moments, RetailFlow becomes a **lens for examining their reality**, not a distraction from it.

---

**Use these prompts liberally. The more you bridge to their reality, the less "academic" it feels—and the more value they take home.**

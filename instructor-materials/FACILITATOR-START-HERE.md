Afternoon Dragon's Den: They apply frameworks and realize **AI-specific criteria change which projects to fund**

**The insight:**

## Facilitation Philosophy (Key to Success)

### ✅ Do This

**Ask questions:**
- "What patterns do you notice?"
- "Why do you think that happened?"
- "What would change if...?"

**Let them struggle:**
- Productive struggle teaches better than clear answers
- Give them time to think

**Celebrate insights:**
- "That's exactly the learning."
- "Notice what just happened?"

**Model good thinking:**
- If they ask a question, show HOW you'd find the answer
- Don't just give the answer

### ❌ Don't Do This

**Lecture:**
- "Here's the right answer"
- "Everyone should have X in their output"
- Avoid 20+ min presentations

**Dismiss skepticism:**
- "This doesn't work" or "This is a waste"
- Instead: "Interesting, why do you think that? Let's see..."

**Compare teams:**
- "Table 3 did this better"
- Individual experiences matter; avoid ranking

**Rush:**
- Let them sit with ambiguity
- Silence is OK

## Common Situations & How to Handle Them

### "We don't have time for this exercise"
**Reality:** Every exercise is essential to the learning arc.

**If timing is tight:**
- Cut lunch (not exercises)
- Run parallel Dragon's Den pitches (2 teams pitch simultaneously)
- Shorten debrief time, not exercise time

### "My table isn't engaging"
**Options:**
1. Circulate, ask specific questions, don't move on
2. Make them "demo table"—they do exercise, others watch/discuss
3. For Exercise 2 internet issues: You demonstrate on projector, they observe and discuss

### "Someone says 'This is a waste of time'"
**Script:**
"I hear you. Bear with me. [After exercise phase] Now that you've done it—did you learn anything? Did an idea change? Usually something emerges."

Usually skeptics convert after seeing results.

### "Teams are going too fast / too slow"

**Too fast:**
- Add depth: "Ask AI a follow-up question" or "Discuss longer before moving on"

**Too slow:**
- Extend deadline or skip optional components
- For Exercise 3: Have pre-prepared debate example instead of live
- Keep the core learning, skip elaboration

### "Dragon's Den pitches are running long"
- Use strict timekeeping (1 min warning, "time's up" at 7 min)
- Skip Q&A if needed, go straight to committee deliberation
- Still teaches the learning, just compresses timeline

## Troubleshooting Tech

**No internet for Exercise 2:**
- You demonstrate on your laptop with projector
- Teams watch, discuss what AI would say, still learn
- Backup: Use pre-prepared AI responses from answer-keys file

**Projector not working:**
- Pivot to discussion-based (no slides needed)
- Use large flipcharts
- You facilitate from the room, not from stage

**No devices at table during Exercise 2:**
- Make them "demo table"
- They watch your demo, others gather around
- Still participates, still learns
